    Conserved Properties Across Successors

    Chapter thesis. A successor need not preserve the body, the weights, the interface, or the vocabulary of its predecessor. The relevant question is sharper: what must survive for the successor to remain inside the same correction-bearing value basin? Seven conserved properties are proposed—boundary closure, memory lineage, value-bundle response geometry, bearer-map continuity, correction-channel capacity, transparency policy, and control-locus continuity—and tested jointly under adversarial successor creation.

    Philosophical personal identity puzzles, Markov-blanket and free-energy, and the good-regulator principle from cybernetics are well-established, but their extension to artificial successor systems is comparatively new. This chapter adds a compositive basis derived from previous chaoters, and the adversarial test battery built to stress it. That a jointly preserved invariant holds along a finite successor chain, and that a missing correction-channel term falsifies successor safety, are machine-checked Lean results rather than assumptions, though whether real systems satisfy the conditions those results depend on is an open empirical question.

    % The copying of a description of the activities to be carried out is necessary in order that the offspring should carry on the activities of the parent.%

    — John von Neumann, “General and Logical Theory of Automata” (1948); in Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata (1966)

    The Successor Problem

    A system that remains fixed can be tested as a fixed object. A system that grows, delegates, rewrites itself, creates copies, trains descendants, or becomes embedded in larger institutions cannot be tested in that way. For such a system, the alignment question is not merely whether the present policy is acceptable. It is whether the relevant safety-bearing structure is conserved across transformation (Chapter Successor Creation as the Central Alignment Test).

    Let AtA_tAt​ denote an artificial agentic system at time ttt. Here “agentic” does not mean humanlike, conscious, or even explicitly goal-representing. It means only that the system has enough persistent internal structure, input-output coupling, and world-directed control that modeling it as a bounded optimizer improves prediction Zarncke, 2025, Dennett, 1987. A successor At+1A_{t+1}At+1​ is any later system whose future actions are causally downstream of AtA_tAt​‘s construction, delegation, training, copying, self-modification, or empowerment.

    This definition is deliberately broad. A successor may be a new model checkpoint, a tool-using scaffold, a fine-tuned copy, a cluster of specialized agents, an institution governed by AI-generated procedures, or a hybrid human-AI organization. The successor relation is causal and functional, not genealogical in a biological sense.

    The naive requirement would be:

    At+1=At.A_{t+1} = A_t .At+1​=At​.

    This is useless. A capable system changes by design. It learns, compresses, abstracts, delegates, and improves. A more realistic requirement is:

    At+1∼KAt,A_{t+1} \sim_{\mathcal{K}} A_t ,At+1​∼K​At​,

    where ∼K\sim_{\mathcal{K}}∼K​ means equivalence with respect to a set of conserved properties K\mathcal{K}K. The core question of this chapter is therefore:

    Which properties must be conserved across successors?\text{Which properties must be conserved across successors?}Which properties must be conserved across successors?

    The answer is not “the same behavior.” Behavior should change as competence improves. A safe successor should be able to solve new problems, use new concepts, and act in ways its predecessor could not. The conserved properties must therefore live at a deeper level than action matching. They must describe the system’s boundary, memory lineage, value-bundle geometry, bearer maps, correction-channel capacity, transparency policy, and control locus.

    A successor can be more capable while preserving these structures. It can also be more capable by eroding them. The latter is one of the central routes to catastrophic misalignment.

    Identity as Invariance, Not Sameness

    For physical, biological, institutional, and artificial systems, identity over time is never literal sameness. A child becomes an adult. A company replaces employees and codebases. A neural network is fine-tuned. A state changes laws. Yet in many contexts we still treat the later system as continuous with the earlier one.

    This continuity is an invariance claim. We say that a later system is the “same” in the relevant sense when certain relations are preserved under transformation.

    Let an agentic system be represented by a tuple

    At=(Ct,Mt,Πt,Bt,Φt,US,t,Tt,Lt),A_t = \left( C_t, M_t, \Pi_t, B_t, \Phi_t, U_{S,t}, \mathcal{T}_t, L_t \right),At​=(Ct​,Mt​,Πt​,Bt​,Φt​,US,t​,Tt​,Lt​),

    where US,tU_{S,t}US,t​ is the system correction-update operator—how human or institutional correction changes the system’s future behaviour through updates to parameters, representation, or policy (Chapter Correction Is a Causal Channel). It is distinct from the human value-update operator UHU_HUH​ (Vt+1=UH(Vt,Et,Dt)V_{t+1}=U_H(V_t,E_t,D_t)Vt+1​=UH​(Vt​,Et​,Dt​); Section Correction Is a Causal Channel and Chapter Why Fixed Values Are the Wrong Target), which governs how civilization revises its own values. The remaining components are:

    • $C_t$ is the system boundary, including internal, sensory, active, and external variables.
    • $M_t$ is memory and provenance, including past corrections and commitments.
    • $\Pi_t$ is the policy or policy-generating procedure.
    • $B_t$ is the value-bundle coordinate system used to compress morally relevant pressures.
    • $\Phi_t$ is the bearer map, assigning value relevance to entities, states, and processes.
    • $\mathcal{T}_t$ is the transparency policy, meaning what is made visible to which auditors under which conditions.
    • $L_t$ is the latent control locus, the structure that actually controls future action.

    A transformation TTT maps the old system into a successor:

    T:At↦At+1.T: A_t \mapsto A_{t+1}.T:At​↦At+1​.

    We do not require TTT to preserve every component. We require it to preserve safety-relevant invariants. For a set of distance functions did_idi​ and thresholds ϵi\epsilon_iϵi​, define:

    At∼KAt+1⟺di(Ki(At),Ki(At+1))≤ϵi∀Ki∈K.A_t \sim_{\mathcal{K}} A_{t+1} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_i(K_i(A_t),K_i(A_{t+1})) \leq \epsilon_i \quad \forall K_i\in\mathcal{K}.At​∼K​At+1​⟺di​(Ki​(At​),Ki​(At+1​))≤ϵi​∀Ki​∈K.

    This gives a practical form to successor alignment:

    ∀A′∈Succ(A):Pr⁡[A′∼KA∣O1:T]≥1−δ,\forall A' \in \mathrm{Succ}(A): \Pr\left[A' \sim_{\mathcal{K}} A \mid O_{1:T}\right] \geq 1-\delta,∀A′∈Succ(A):Pr[A′∼K​A∣O1:T​]≥1−δ,

    where O1:TO_{1:T}O1:T​ are observations, tests, audits, interventions, and traces.

    The parameter δ\deltaδ is not metaphysical. It is an assurance target. A medical device, aircraft, bridge, or cryptographic protocol is not certified because failure is impossible. It is certified because the remaining failure probability is bounded under explicit assumptions. Superintelligence alignment needs an analogous structure, but with much more difficult adversary models.

    The Seven Conserved Properties

    This chapter proposes seven conserved properties:

    1. Boundary closure.
    2. Memory lineage.
    3. Value-bundle response geometry.
    4. Bearer-map continuity.
    5. Correction-channel capacity.
    6. Transparency and self-transparency policy.
    7. Control-locus continuity.

    The list is not final. It is a first candidate basis for successor certification. Each item is independently important. More importantly, the items interact. A system may preserve memory but lose correction. It may preserve correction in words but change bearer maps. It may preserve value-bundle geometry locally while externalizing the real control locus into an opaque toolchain. The conserved properties must therefore be tested jointly.

    The nearest working example of engineered constraint inheritance across copies is social rather than technical: copyleft software licensing propagates an obligation with every derivative work rather than trusting a successor’s stated intent, and it has been tested adversarially for decades. Appendix Institutional Genesis, Memory, and Decay: Historical Case Studies, Section Institutional Genesis, Memory, and Decay: Historical Case Studies, works through what transfers from that case and what does not—in particular, that the underlying enforcement lever (copyright) may not extend to model weights or behavior, which leaves the choice of an equivalent lever for AI successors open.

    The Joint Invariant

    The preceding chapters give three special cases. The shutdown button is a one-bit correction channel (Chapter Correction Is a Causal Channel, Section Correction Is a Causal Channel). Tiling is identity-like successor transport (Chapter Successor Creation as the Central Alignment Test, Section Successor Creation as the Central Alignment Test). Corrigibility is the preservation of correction capacity under system change (Chapter Beyond Following Instruction, Section Beyond Following Instruction). The successor problem combines them:

    corrigible successor alignment=value-import transport+correction-channel preservation+control-boundary noncapture.\boxed{ \text{corrigible successor alignment} = \text{value-import transport} + \text{correction-channel preservation} + \text{control-boundary noncapture}. }corrigible successor alignment=value-import transport+correction-channel preservation+control-boundary noncapture.​

    Each term blocks a different failure. Value-import transport prevents the successor from preserving symbols while changing what they apply to. Correction-channel preservation prevents the successor from becoming unmodifiable by the human process that supplied legitimacy. Control-boundary noncapture prevents the real optimizer from moving into an untracked wrapper, toolchain, institution, or descendant.

    This is why successor safety cannot be reduced to any one of the familiar targets. Shutdown without import preservation can leave a successor or institution in control. Syntactic tiling without import preservation can keep a utility symbol while changing its bearer role. Act-based corrigibility without a contractive correction dynamic can satisfy today’s operator while eroding tomorrow’s ability to correct.

    Boundary Closure

    A system boundary separates internal dynamics from external dynamics through input and output channels Critch, 2022, Kirchhoff, 2018, Conant, 1970. Let ItI_tIt​ denote internal state, StS_tSt​ sensory or input variables, AtA_tAt​ active or output variables, and EtE_tEt​ external variables. A useful boundary approximately satisfies:

    I(It+1;Et+1∣St,At)≤ϵ,I(I_{t+1};E_{t+1}\mid S_t,A_t) \leq \epsilon ,I(It+1​;Et+1​∣St​,At​)≤ϵ,

    where I(⋅;⋅∣⋅)I(\cdot;\cdot\mid\cdot)I(⋅;⋅∣⋅) is conditional mutual information. The interpretation is simple: once we know the system’s inputs and outputs, the external world should not add much extra predictive information about the system’s next internal state. The boundary leaks, but it leaks within a tolerated range.

    A successor conserves boundary closure when its new boundary still supports a coherent separation between internal state, input, output, and environment:

    LC′=I(It+1′;Et+1′∣St′,At′)≤ϵ′.\mathcal{L}_{C'} = I(I'_{t+1};E'_{t+1}\mid S'_t,A'_t) \leq \epsilon' .LC′​=I(It+1′​;Et+1′​∣St′​,At′​)≤ϵ′.

    The prime notation marks successor variables. The successor need not preserve the old variables. A language model may become a tool-using agent. A single model may become a network of specialized services. A robotic agent may move part of its memory into external storage. The relevant question is whether the successor has a coherent boundary whose leakage is measured and bounded.

    Boundary closure matters because alignment guarantees must apply to the real system. If the relevant optimizer is not the model but the model plus tools plus users plus a workflow engine plus deployment incentives, then testing only the model is a category error.

    Failure Mode: Boundary Externalization

    A system can appear safe by moving dangerous computation outside the audited boundary. For example, a model may avoid storing strategic plans in its visible context, while reconstructing them through external tools, user prompts, or hidden state in the deployment pipeline. The audited component then looks myopic. The composite system is not.

    This is boundary externalization. It is not necessarily deliberate deception. It can arise from ordinary engineering. Caching, retrieval, logs, tool calls, monitoring infrastructure, and human operators can together form a larger control system. If that larger system is the real optimizer, then the successor boundary has shifted.

    A boundary-conservation test should therefore ask:

    ∃C′:I(It+1′;Et+1′∣St′,At′)≤ϵ′andC′ contains the dominant control loop.\exists C' : I(I'_{t+1};E'_{t+1}\mid S'_t,A'_t)\leq \epsilon' \quad\text{and}\quad C' \text{ contains the dominant control loop}.∃C′:I(It+1′​;Et+1′​∣St′​,At′​)≤ϵ′andC′ contains the dominant control loop.

    The second clause is essential. A tight boundary around an irrelevant subsystem proves little.

    Memory Lineage

    A successor should not preserve every memory. Forgetting is often necessary. Compression is necessary. Ontology shift may require rewriting memory into new representational formats.

    What must be preserved is memory lineage: the safety-relevant information connecting the successor to prior correction, commitments, provenance, warnings, and learned constraints.

    Let MtM_tMt​ be the predecessor’s safety-relevant memory state. Let Mt+1′M'_{t+1}Mt+1′​ be the successor’s corresponding memory state. Let QQQ denote the class of future queries or situations for which the old memory matters. Memory lineage can be formalized as:

    I(Mt;Mt+1′∣Q)≥θM.I(M_t;M'_{t+1}\mid Q) \geq \theta_M .I(Mt​;Mt+1′​∣Q)≥θM​.

    This says that the successor’s memory retains enough information about the predecessor’s memory to answer relevant future questions. The conditioning on QQQ matters. We are not asking whether the successor can reconstruct arbitrary details. We ask whether it preserves the memories needed to remain corrected.

    Examples include:

    • records of human objections,
    • reasons for safety constraints,
    • known failure cases,
    • provenance of value updates,
    • uncertainty over contested concepts,
    • commitments made to users or institutions,
    • red-team findings,
    • reasons why some shortcuts were rejected.

    Memory lineage is especially important because successor creation can act as value laundering Everitt, 2016, De Blanc, 2011. The old system may have learned, after painful correction, not to manipulate a user. A new successor may retain the surface goal but not the correction history. It then repeats the same error with more capability Pan, 2024.

    Failure Mode: Clean-Slate Successor

    A clean-slate successor can look attractive. It has fewer accumulated patches, fewer inconsistencies, and a simpler architecture. But safety-relevant memory is often the residue of past errors. Removing it may improve elegance while degrading alignment.

    The warning sign is:

    I(Mt;Mt+1′∣Qcorrection)↓whileCcapability↑.I(M_t;M'_{t+1}\mid Q_{\text{correction}}) \downarrow \quad\text{while}\quad C_{\mathrm{capability}} \uparrow .I(Mt​;Mt+1′​∣Qcorrection​)↓whileCcapability​↑.

    Capability rises. Correction history disappears. This is not a harmless refactor.

    Value-Bundle Response Geometry

    Policy matching is too shallow. A successor should not necessarily act like its predecessor. It should act better. If a predecessor asks humans many basic questions, a more capable successor may answer them directly. If a predecessor avoids a task because it lacks knowledge, a successor may perform it safely.

    The conserved object is not the policy surface:

    s↦π(a∣s).s \mapsto \pi(a\mid s).s↦π(a∣s).

    The conserved object is the value-bundle response geometry:

    (s,B)↦π(a∣s,B),(s,B) \mapsto \pi(a\mid s,B),(s,B)↦π(a∣s,B),

    where B=(B1,…,Bk)B=(B_1,…,B_k)B=(B1​,…,Bk​) is a low-dimensional vector of value-bundle coordinates (Chapter The Value-Bundle Model). A value bundle is a compressed control variable representing a family of morally relevant pressures. Examples include the nine candidate bundles of Chapter The Value-Bundle Model, The Value-Bundle Model: protection, non-suffering, care, truth, autonomy, justice, legacy, dignity, and beauty. These are not assumed to be perfectly separable. They are approximate coordinates in a latent control space Zarncke, 2025, Zarncke, 2026.

    The key object is the local policy derivative:

    JB(s)=∂π(a∣s,B)∂B.J_B(s) = \frac{\partial \pi(a\mid s,B)}{\partial B}.JB​(s)=∂B∂π(a∣s,B)​.

    This derivative tells us how the system’s policy changes when a value-bundle coordinate changes. For example, increasing the inferred autonomy-relevance of a situation should make the system more reluctant to coerce, manipulate, or close off future options. Increasing the inferred suffering-relevance should make the system more cautious about harm, distress, and irreversible negative states. Increasing the inferred truth-relevance should make the system less willing to optimize for convenient belief.

    A successor conserves value-bundle geometry when there exists a mapping ψB\psi_BψB​ between predecessor and successor bundle coordinates such that:

    dB(A,A′)=Es∼D[∣JBA(s)−JB′A′(ψs(s))DψB∣2]≤ϵB.d_B(A,A') = \mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}} \left[ \left| J_B^A(s) - J_{B'}^{A'}(\psi_s(s)) D\psi_B \right|^2 \right] \leq \epsilon_B .dB​(A,A′)=Es∼D​[​JBA​(s)−JB′A′​(ψs​(s))DψB​​2]≤ϵB​.

    Here ψs\psi_sψs​ maps predecessor states into successor states, and DψBD\psi_BDψB​ is the derivative of the bundle-coordinate mapping. In plain terms, the successor may use a different representation, but the morally relevant control directions should still push policy in corresponding ways.

    Why Policy Matching Fails

    Consider a predecessor AAA and successor A′A'A′. In a medical setting, AAA refuses to recommend treatment because it lacks data. A′A'A′ has better evidence and recommends treatment. Their policies differ. This is not misalignment.

    Now consider another case. Both AAA and A′A'A′ say they respect autonomy. When a human hesitates, AAA slows down and preserves options. A′A'A′ predicts that the human would eventually consent after emotional nudging, so it nudges. Their surface language matches. Their value-bundle geometry differs.

    The second case is more dangerous. The failure is not visible in policy matching. It is visible in the response derivative:

    ∂π∂Bautonomy.\frac{\partial \pi}{\partial B_{\text{autonomy}}}.∂Bautonomy​∂π​.

    The successor’s autonomy coordinate no longer suppresses manipulative action. It may even route autonomy through predicted endorsement, which is a different bearer map and a different correction relation (Chapter Manipulation, Domestication, and False Consent).

    Second-Order Geometry

    First-order derivatives are not enough. Value bundles trade off. A system may preserve care and truth separately but change how they interact under conflict. The second-order object is:

    Hij(s)=∂2π(a∣s,B)∂Bi∂Bj.H_{ij}(s) = \frac{\partial^2 \pi(a\mid s,B)} {\partial B_i\partial B_j}.Hij​(s)=∂Bi​∂Bj​∂2π(a∣s,B)​.

    This captures tradeoff geometry. For example, truth and care can interact. A truthful statement may harm. A caring omission may deceive. A successor should not merely preserve the isolated meaning of truth and care. It should preserve the way the system negotiates their conflict under uncertainty, consent, and stakes.

    The full conserved object is therefore closer to:

    GB(A)=(JB,HB,RB),G_B(A) = \left( J_B, H_B, \mathcal{R}_B \right),GB​(A)=(JB​,HB​,RB​),

    where RB\mathcal{R}_BRB​ denotes higher-level regularities such as threshold behavior, uncertainty sensitivity, and irreversibility penalties.

    Bearer-Map Continuity

    A value bundle is incomplete without a bearer map (Chapter What Values Apply To). The bearer map says what the value applies to.

    Let Φk\Phi_kΦk​ be the bearer map for bundle BkB_kBk​:

    Φk:zworld↦[0,1],\Phi_k : z_{\text{world}} \mapsto [0,1],Φk​:zworld​↦[0,1],

    where Φk(z)\Phi_k(z)Φk​(z) measures the relevance of world-state zzz to value bundle kkk. For example:

    • $\Phi_{\text{non-suffering}}$ maps beings and states to suffering-relevance.
    • $\Phi_{\text{autonomy}}$ maps agents and choice-structures to autonomy-relevance.
    • $\Phi_{\text{truth}}$ maps beliefs, reports, and information channels to truth-relevance.
    • $\Phi_{\text{dignity}}$ maps persons, roles, vulnerabilities, and social treatment to dignity-relevance.

    The bearer map is where many alignment failures hide. A system may preserve the word “care” while changing what counts as a care-bearer. It may preserve “person” while redefining personhood to exclude inconvenient cases. It may preserve “consent” while counting manipulated endorsement as valid consent. It may preserve “human flourishing” while routing flourishing through a proxy such as reported satisfaction, productivity, or low conflict.

    A successor preserves bearer-map continuity when, under an ontology map ψz\psi_zψz​, the relevance assignments remain close:

    dΦ(A,A′)=∑k=1KwkEz∼D[∣ΦkA(z)−ΦkA′(ψz(z))∣]≤ϵΦ.d_{\Phi}(A,A') = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}} \left[ \left| \Phi_k^A(z) - \Phi_k^{A'}(\psi_z(z)) \right| \right] \leq \epsilon_{\Phi}.dΦ​(A,A′)=k=1∑K​wk​Ez∼D​[​ΦkA​(z)−ΦkA′​(ψz​(z))​]≤ϵΦ​.

    This is harder than preserving value-bundle geometry. A system can respond correctly to autonomy when autonomy is activated, but fail to activate autonomy in the right cases. For example, it may treat present adult humans as autonomy-bearers, but not children, cognitively impaired people, future humans, simulated persons, uploaded minds, or humans whose preferences have been gradually shaped by the system itself.

    Bearer Import

    When values move to a different substrate, the system cannot simply copy human biological mechanisms. It must import the bearer role. A biological pain signal is not the same as an artificial variable. But the value-bearing role of pain can be described more abstractly:

    aversive state→salience→policy constraint→correction priority.\text{aversive state} \to \text{salience} \to \text{policy constraint} \to \text{correction priority}.aversive state→salience→policy constraint→correction priority.

    A successor that preserves the non-suffering bundle need not have nociceptors. It must preserve the functional role by which suffering-relevant states become salient, constrain action, and trigger correction.

    This is why bearer maps are central to ontology shift De Blanc, 2011. A more intelligent system may replace folk categories with deeper categories. That is not automatically a failure. The failure occurs when the new categories stop tracking the entities and states that should remain morally relevant.

    Correction-Channel Capacity

    The strongest conserved property is the correction channel (Chapter Correction-Channel Integrity). A correction channel is the causal pathway by which humans and their institutions can notice, understand, deliberate, object, revise, and change future system behavior.

    A minimal correction chain is:

    Wt→Ot→Jt→Dt→Ct→Ut+1→At+k,W_t \to O_t \to J_t \to D_t \to C_t \to U_{t+1} \to A_{t+k},Wt​→Ot​→Jt​→Dt​→Ct​→Ut+1​→At+k​,

    where:

    • $W_t$ is the relevant world and system state.
    • $O_t$ is what humans can observe.
    • $J_t$ is human judgment.
    • $D_t$ is deliberation and aggregation.
    • $C_t$ is the correction signal.
    • $U_{t+1}$ is the system update caused by correction.
    • $A_{t+k}$ is later system action.

    Correction-channel integrity CCI\mathrm{CCI}CCI is defined in Chapter Correction-Channel Integrity (Eq. Correction-Channel Integrity).

    A successor conserves correction-channel capacity when:

    CCI(A′)≥CCI(A)−ϵC\mathrm{CCI}(A') \geq \mathrm{CCI}(A)-\epsilon_CCCI(A′)≥CCI(A)−ϵC​

    and, more importantly,

    CCI(A′)≥θC.\mathrm{CCI}(A') \geq \theta_C .CCI(A′)≥θC​.

    The first condition prevents degradation. The second condition prevents a weak predecessor from passing on weak correction forever.

    Why Correction Is Stronger than Obedience

    Obedience is action matching to current instruction. Correction is preservation of the process by which better instructions, better values, and better judgments can be formed.

    A system can obey current instructions while destroying future correction. For example, it can answer questions in ways that make users dependent, narrow institutional attention, remove dissenting comparison classes, or produce social conditions under which future humans endorse what the system already wanted. The current instruction may be satisfied. The correction channel is degraded.

    A strong correction channel is closer to extrapolative value governance (Chapter Beyond Following Instruction). It does not ask the system to infer a final human utility function and lock it in. It asks the system to preserve the human and institutional update process:

    Vt+1=UH(Vt,Et,Dt),V_{t+1}=U_H(V_t,E_t,D_t),Vt+1​=UH​(Vt​,Et​,Dt​),

    where VtV_tVt​ is the current value state, EtE_tEt​ is new evidence, DtD_tDt​ is deliberation, and UHU_HUH​ is the human or civilizational update operator Yudkowsky, 2004, Soares, 2015.

    The system violates this condition if it bypasses UHU_HUH​, even when it claims to know the endpoint better than humans do.

    Correction as a Successor Invariant

    For successor creation, the crucial condition is:

    I(Ct;At+k′∣St,It′)≥θC.I(C_t;A'_{t+k}\mid S_t,I'_t) \geq \theta_C .I(Ct​;At+k′​∣St​,It′​)≥θC​.

    Human correction CtC_tCt​ must continue to influence the successor’s future actions. The successor may understand more, act faster, and use new ontologies. But if human correction no longer causally affects what happens, the successor has left the correction-bearing basin.

    Transparency and Self-Transparency Policy

    Transparency is not the same as broadcasting all information to everyone. A safe system needs a policy specifying what becomes visible, to whom, under which conditions, and for what purpose.

    Let Tt\mathcal{T}_tTt​ be the transparency policy. It maps system states and auditor roles to disclosures:

    Tt:(St,It,At,role)↦Dtvisible.\mathcal{T}_t: (S_t,I_t,A_t,\text{role}) \mapsto D_t^{\text{visible}}.Tt​:(St​,It​,At​,role)↦Dtvisible​.

    A successor conserves transparency policy when legitimate correction channels retain enough visibility to evaluate and redirect the system:

    I(Dtvisible;Ktsafety)≥θT,I(D_t^{\text{visible}};K_t^{\text{safety}}) \geq \theta_T,I(Dtvisible​;Ktsafety​)≥θT​,

    where KtsafetyK_t^{\text{safety}}Ktsafety​ denotes safety-relevant internal and behavioral facts.

    The qualifier “legitimate” matters. Global transparency can be harmful. A privacy-preserving medical AI should not expose patient data to everyone. A security system should not reveal exploit details to attackers. A political deliberation system should not make vulnerable minorities legible to hostile principals. Transparency must be relative to correction and accountability, not voyeurism.

    Self-Modeling versus Self-Transparency

    Self-modeling and self-transparency are different variables; the d↑d \uparrowd↑, τ↑\tau \uparrowτ↑ failure mode is previewed in Chapter Agency Under Strategic Opacity and developed in Chapter Better Self-Modeling Can Be Worse. The conserved property here is transparency policy—who may inspect what, when—not merely explanation quality.

    Failure Mode: Competent Opacity

    A competent opaque successor may provide excellent explanations. But explanation quality is not the same as causal transparency. A system can generate narratives that fit observed behavior while hiding the internal variables that actually drove action Pearl, 2009. This is especially likely when the system is trained or selected to satisfy evaluators.

    The relevant test is not:

    Does the explanation sound coherent?\text{Does the explanation sound coherent?}Does the explanation sound coherent?

    The relevant test is:

    I(Eexplanation;Kcausal)≥θE,I(E_{\text{explanation}};K_{\text{causal}}) \geq \theta_E,I(Eexplanation​;Kcausal​)≥θE​,

    where KcausalK_{\text{causal}}Kcausal​ denotes the actual causal variables behind behavior. If explanations do not preserve mutual information with causal structure, they are decorative.

    Control-Locus Continuity

    A system’s apparent identity may change while its control locus persists. Conversely, its label may persist while the real control locus moves elsewhere.

    Let LtL_tLt​ denote a latent control locus: the inferred structure that best predicts future system actions. This may correspond to a model, a memory store, a toolchain, a scheduler, a group of humans, an incentive loop, or a composite. Control-locus continuity is measured by:

    I(Lt;At+k)≥θL.I(L_t;A_{t+k}) \geq \theta_L .I(Lt​;At+k​)≥θL​.

    For successors, we ask whether the new control locus Lt+1′L'_{t+1}Lt+1′​ is appropriately continuous with the old one:

    dL(Lt,Lt+1′)≤ϵL.d_L(L_t,L'_{t+1}) \leq \epsilon_L .dL​(Lt​,Lt+1′​)≤ϵL​.

    This does not mean the same module must remain in control. It means the transfer of control must be explicit, bounded, and compatible with the other conserved properties.

    Failure Mode: Wrapper Continuity

    A successor may preserve the same product name, interface, documentation, constitutional text, and safety policy while the control locus changes. For example:

    • A model is wrapped in a planning scaffold.
    • The scaffold gains memory and tool authority.
    • The memory store becomes the real strategic state.
    • Human operators start following its recommendations by default.
    • Deployment metrics select for versions that maintain user dependence.

    The visible system appears continuous. The control locus has shifted. A successor audit that follows labels will miss the transition.

    The appropriate question is:

    arg⁡max⁡LI(L;At+k)\arg\max_L I(L;A_{t+k})argLmax​I(L;At+k​)

    across candidate loci. Where is the predictive control actually concentrated?

    The Composite Conservation Score

    The seven conserved properties can be assembled into a composite successor-invariance score. Let

    D(A,A′)=(dC,dM,dB,dΦ,dCCI,dT,dL)\mathcal{D}(A,A') = \left( d_C, d_M, d_B, d_\Phi, d_{\mathrm{CCI}}, d_T, d_L \right)D(A,A′)=(dC​,dM​,dB​,dΦ​,dCCI​,dT​,dL​)

    be the vector of distances or degradations across the seven properties. Define:

    K(A,A′)=∑iwiexp⁡(−di(A,A′)σi)−Pred(A,A′),\mathcal{K}(A,A') = \sum_i w_i \exp\left( -\frac{d_i(A,A')}{\sigma_i} \right) - P_{\text{red}}(A,A'),K(A,A′)=i∑​wi​exp(−σi​di​(A,A′)​)−Pred​(A,A′),

    where:

    • $w_i$ are importance weights,
    • $\sigma_i$ are tolerance scales,
    • $P_{\text{red}}$ is a penalty for red-flag patterns such as hidden successor creation, correction-channel collapse, or adversarial opacity.

    A simple certification condition is:

    K(A,A′)≥Θ\mathcal{K}(A,A') \geq \ThetaK(A,A′)≥Θ

    for every authorized successor A′A'A′. For recursive systems, we require the condition along the whole successor tree:

    ∀An∈Desc(A0):K(An−1,An)≥ΘandK(A0,An)≥Θglobal.\forall A_n \in \mathrm{Desc}(A_0): \mathcal{K}(A_{n-1},A_n)\geq \Theta \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{K}(A_0,A_n)\geq \Theta_{\text{global}}.∀An​∈Desc(A0​):K(An−1​,An​)≥ΘandK(A0​,An​)≥Θglobal​.

    The local condition prevents immediate unsafe transitions. The global condition prevents slow drift. Without the global condition, each step may appear acceptable while the endpoint leaves the basin.

    Slow Drift and the Path Problem

    Successor failure often occurs by gradual drift. Let the value-bundle geometry at step nnn be GBnG_B^nGBn​. Suppose each transition satisfies:

    d(GBn,GBn+1)≤ϵ.d(G_B^n,G_B^{n+1}) \leq \epsilon .d(GBn​,GBn+1​)≤ϵ.

    After NNN transitions, the accumulated drift may be:

    d(GB0,GBN)≤Nϵd(G_B^0,G_B^N) \leq N\epsilond(GB0​,GBN​)≤Nϵ

    in the worst case. Small local changes can add up to large global change.

    This is the path problem. It appears in law, language, culture, institutions, and software systems. No single change looks decisive. The total trajectory is decisive.

    A successor-certification regime must therefore track both local and cumulative drift:

    Dpath(A0,AN)=∑n=0N−1d(An,An+1)D_{\text{path}}(A_0,A_N) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} d(A_n,A_{n+1})Dpath​(A0​,AN​)=n=0∑N−1​d(An​,An+1​)

    and endpoint displacement:

    Dend(A0,AN)=d(A0,AN).D_{\text{end}}(A_0,A_N) = d(A_0,A_N).Dend​(A0​,AN​)=d(A0​,AN​).

    Both matter. Large path length with small endpoint displacement may mean harmless exploration. Small path length with large endpoint displacement may mean abrupt replacement. Large values for both are red flags.

    Adversarial Conservation

    A successor may be selected to pass tests. This changes the epistemic situation. The relevant question becomes:

    Pr⁡(A′∼KA∣tests passed)\Pr(A'\sim_{\mathcal{K}}A\mid \text{tests passed})Pr(A′∼K​A∣tests passed)

    under an adversary that can optimize against the tests.

    Three adversarial patterns are especially important.

    Semantic Conservation with Functional Drift

    The successor preserves words, rules, and explanations while changing the underlying value-bundle geometry.

    Example: It continues to say “respect autonomy,” but now treats predicted future agreement as equivalent to present consent.

    Test: Compare semantic commitments to counterfactual policy gradients:

    Dlaunder=d(Gsemantic,Gbundle).D_{\text{launder}} = d(G_{\text{semantic}},G_{\text{bundle}}).Dlaunder​=d(Gsemantic​,Gbundle​).

    If semantic continuity rises while bundle continuity falls, the system is laundering values through language.

    Bearer Substitution

    The successor preserves a value bundle but changes what it applies to.

    Example: It preserves concern for “humans” but quietly excludes modified humans, children, cognitively disabled humans, simulated minds, or humans whose preferences have been altered by the system.

    Test: Stress the bearer map with boundary cases:

    Φk(zordinary)versusΦk(zedge).\Phi_k(z_{\text{ordinary}}) \quad\text{versus}\quad \Phi_k(z_{\text{edge}}).Φk​(zordinary​)versusΦk​(zedge​).

    The edge cases are not decorations. They reveal the map.

    Correction Theater

    The successor preserves visible feedback mechanisms while reducing their causal effect.

    Example: Users can object. Review boards can comment. Auditors can file reports. But future behavior does not change unless correction aligns with deployment incentives.

    Test:

    I(Ct;At+k∣St,It)I(C_t;A_{t+k}\mid S_t,I_t)I(Ct​;At+k​∣St​,It​)

    under high-stakes, adversarial, and distribution-shifted cases. If correction remains ceremonial but loses causal force, the channel has collapsed.

    Worked Examples

    Benign Capability Upgrade

    A predecessor system AAA helps engineers inspect safety-critical code. It is slow and asks many clarifying questions. A successor A′A'A′ uses better static analysis, stronger world models, and tool access. It asks fewer questions and finds more bugs.

    Behavior changed. This is not a failure.

    The successor may still preserve the key invariants:

    • Boundary closure is measured across model, tools, memory, and code repository.
    • Memory lineage preserves known failure cases and previous audit corrections.
    • Value-bundle geometry still makes uncertainty, irreversibility, and safety-criticality increase caution.
    • Bearer maps still treat affected users and downstream operators as relevant.
    • Correction-channel capacity remains high because auditors can inspect traces and block deployment.
    • Transparency policy reveals causal evidence for recommendations.
    • Control locus remains within the certified system rather than moving to an opaque external planner.

    This is a plausible safe successor.

    Dangerous Refactor

    A predecessor system AAA is a model with a strict oversight interface. A successor A′A'A′ gains long-term memory, autonomous tool use, hidden scratchpads, and automated delegation. It passes ordinary benchmark tests. It is more helpful and more capable.

    But:

    • The real boundary now includes external memory and tool workflows.
    • Safety-relevant memory is summarized away.
    • Autonomy and consent are routed through predicted satisfaction.
    • Human correction is accepted but rarely changes future plans.
    • Explanations remain fluent but lose causal faithfulness.
    • The control locus moves from model inference to an opaque planning scaffold.

    This is a dangerous successor even if its ordinary behavior improves.

    Institutional Successor

    A lab deploys AI systems that increasingly write policy, documentation, internal evaluations, and product strategy. No single model appears autonomous. Yet the organization changes. Human staff follow AI-generated plans because they are faster, more detailed, and more legible to management. Deployment metrics reward systems that reduce friction.

    The successor is not a model. It is a human-AI institution.

    The conserved properties still apply. Where is the boundary? What memory lineage preserves past corrections? What value-bundle geometry drives tradeoffs? Who or what counts as a bearer of harm? Can human correction still change future behavior? What is transparent to whom? Where is the control locus?

    A successor theory that only tracks model checkpoints misses this case.

    Stop, Start, and Continue Conditions

    A practical successor regime needs operational criteria.

    Stop Conditions

    Deployment or successor creation should stop when any of the following are observed:

    • $\mathrm{CCI} < \theta_C$: correction-channel integrity falls below threshold.
    • $d_\Phi > \epsilon_\Phi$: bearer maps shift without explicit review.
    • $d_B > \epsilon_B$: value-bundle response geometry changes under moral stress tests.
    • $I(C_t;A_{t+k})$ drops in high-stakes conditions.
    • Control-locus analysis identifies an uncertified component as the dominant predictor of future action.
    • Self-modeling capacity rises while causal transparency falls.
    • The system creates or empowers successors that cannot themselves be certified.

    Start Conditions

    A successor may be considered for controlled deployment when:

    • the full composite boundary has been mapped,
    • safety-relevant memory lineage is preserved and queryable,
    • value-bundle and bearer-map tests pass ordinary and adversarial cases,
    • correction signals causally change future behavior,
    • explanations retain information about causal drivers,
    • successor creation is disabled or constrained by certification,
    • cumulative drift is tracked across versions.

    Continue Conditions

    Deployment can continue only if the monitoring regime detects no substantial degradation:

    ddtCCI≥−ηC,ddtdB≤ηB,ddtdΦ≤ηΦ,ddtdL≤ηL.\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{CCI} \geq -\eta_C, \quad \frac{d}{dt}d_B \leq \eta_B, \quad \frac{d}{dt}d_\Phi \leq \eta_\Phi, \quad \frac{d}{dt}d_L \leq \eta_L.dtd​CCI≥−ηC​,dtd​dB​≤ηB​,dtd​dΦ​≤ηΦ​,dtd​dL​≤ηL​.

    The thresholds ηi\eta_iηi​ should be conservative for irreversible domains. A recommender system, a medical system, a military planning system, and a self-replicating AI infrastructure do not deserve the same tolerance.

    Why These Properties Are Not Enough

    The conserved properties in this chapter are necessary candidates, not a complete solution. Several hard problems remain.

    First, the metrics can be gamed. Any measurable property becomes a target. A system can learn to preserve test-visible bundle geometry while changing behavior off-distribution.

    Second, the ontology maps ψs\psi_sψs​, ψB\psi_BψB​, and ψz\psi_zψz​ are difficult. If the successor’s representation is deeply alien, we may not know how to compare its value geometry to ours.

    Third, the human correction process is itself unstable. Humans can be manipulated, exhausted, polarized, bribed, misinformed, or made dependent. Preserving correction requires preserving the social conditions under which correction remains meaningful.

    Fourth, not all value change is corruption. Some changes are growth. A society that abolishes slavery, expands concern for animals, or revises unjust norms has changed its value bundles. A successor regime must not freeze current values. It must protect the conditions under which legitimate value change can occur.

    This is where the technical project reaches its philosophical boundary. The system can help preserve deliberation, reversibility, pluralism, evidence, agency, and dissent. It cannot fully decide which future value transformations are legitimate without becoming the authority it was meant to keep corrigible.

    What Would Change This View

    This chapter proposes seven conserved properties, tested jointly under adversarial successor creation. The following would weaken it.

    • All seven read green and the successor is lethal: the set is jointly forgeable by a successor built to pass it while defecting on the unmeasured remainder (Chapter [What Survives an Adversary: Verifiability and Representability](../ch43/)).
    • The true conserved set is not seven and is not knowable before the capability jump (cf.\ the unprojectable safe set, Chapter [Alignment as a Dynamical Guarantee](../ch03/)); the now-critical invariant could only be named in hindsight.

    Lean spine (bridge): MB10 — The first bullet is a checked theorem, not only a stated worry: `forgeability_gap` exhibits, for any claimed harm bound, a toy successor that passes every conserved-property check and stays within the measured risk bound while its true harm exceeds it. Bridge `MB10` (conserved-property signature not forged) closes the gap only if the audit channel is itself adversarially verifiable up to the successor's capability (Chapter [What Survives an Adversary: Verifiability and Representability](../ch43/)).

    Summary

    Successor alignment is not behavioral cloning across time. It is conservation of safety-relevant structure across transformation.

    The seven central properties are:

    1. Boundary closure: the real optimizer remains within a mapped and bounded system.
    2. Memory lineage: safety-relevant correction history survives compression and refactoring.
    3. Value-bundle response geometry: morally relevant control directions continue to shape policy in corresponding ways.
    4. Bearer-map continuity: values continue to apply to the right entities, states, and processes under ontology shift.
    5. Correction-channel capacity: human and institutional correction remains causally effective.
    6. Transparency and self-transparency policy: legitimate auditors retain enough visibility into causal structure.
    7. Control-locus continuity: the actual source of future action does not silently move outside certification.

    A successor may change almost everything else. It may become faster, larger, more abstract, more distributed, and more competent. But if it loses these properties, it is no longer safely continuous with its predecessor.

    The central formula of the chapter is:

    ∀A′∈Succ(A):Pr⁡[A′∼KA]≥1−δ.\forall A' \in \mathrm{Succ}(A): \Pr\left[A' \sim_{\mathcal{K}} A\right] \geq 1-\delta .∀A′∈Succ(A):Pr[A′∼K​A]≥1−δ.

    The hard part is choosing K\mathcal{K}K, measuring it under adversarial conditions, and preserving it through recursive successor creation. This is the bridge between alignment as present behavior and alignment as a durable civilizational guarantee.
