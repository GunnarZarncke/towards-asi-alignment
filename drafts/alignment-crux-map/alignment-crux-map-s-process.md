# Alignment Crux Map (S-process, $50k cap)

Same project as [`alignment-crux-map.md`](alignment-crux-map.md). **Cap $50,000.** **12 steps.** **Minimum:** steps 1–2 (correctable-AI lab + Redwood). No Apollo. MATS and BlueDot are use tests (existing contacts). One of **CHAI or Christiano**, not both.

**Lead:** Gunnar Zarncke (aintelope / Towards Superintelligence Alignment).

Utilities are **our relative judgment**, not measured impact. Unit: **1 U** = one avoided year of a capable person, or one funding decision, aimed at the wrong job under a crowded word. Ratios matter; do not compare these U to another org without a shared reference.

**Funded words** (the claim in the description is true of these): corrigibility, shutdown, deception, evals, tiling, inner alignment. People already fund and enter under those names. This grant splits the jobs under those names.

**Not funded as words:** coverage, selection, oversight, substitution, who-acts. Real lab splits exist; they are not crowded labels a funder would check a proposal against, and a newcomer is not about to spend a year on “coverage.” Capture and re-route can still sit on the workplace as mechanics of shutdown/corrigibility.

## Paste-ready fields

**Name:** Alignment Crux Map

**One-liner:** A public, object-level map of AI-safety work so funders, researchers, and people entering the field can see which real problems are actually being worked on, and which only share a name.

**Description:**

Org lists tell you who exists. This project tells you *which problem a dollar or a year of work is actually buying*.

Funders use it to check a proposal against the problem they thought they were funding. Researchers use it to see which gap their method addresses. People entering the field use it so they do not spend a year on a crowded label and miss an empty job.

That claim is only true for **corrigibility, shutdown, deception, evals, tiling, and inner alignment.** Those are the words this grant freezes.

**Minimum ($15,500):** freeze the **correctable-AI lab** (corrigibility, shutdown, deception, evals on one workplace) and pay Redwood to run *their* setup on it. LLM assistants for that stretch are in the number. Example: honor-the-hold can stop deploy (shutdown) and still leave the correction ticket fake.

If funded further: MATS and BlueDot mentors run the corrigibility table on real abstracts (two training pipelines); CHAI (or Christiano if CHAI cannot) runs the correctable-AI lab on shutdown vs corrigibility; then tiling, inner alignment, missed-lines, Wentworth / Garrabrant / Kosoy, light upkeep.

Who is doing it. Gunnar Zarncke, founder of [aintelope](https://www.aintelope.net/). Drafting is AI-assisted under his direction and review. aintelope people freeze lab configs; that does not count as independent validation. The MATS and BlueDot use tests are training-program mentors, not aintelope.

Where it is up to. A public draft map and a simulated lab already exist. This grant does not buy a new book or a new lab.

S-process can stop after the correctable-AI lab + Redwood. Extra words are later increments.

**Theory of impact:**

Powerful AI can fail in ways that do not show up as a bad eval score. Humans can no longer correct the system. A replacement system can drop the rules. The environment can reward systems that look aligned on the checked channel and defect elsewhere.

Those are specific jobs. If the field funds “corrigibility” and that word means three different jobs, a portfolio can look full and still have an empty job.

1. Money and newcomers follow cluster names (the six words above).
2. The same name can mean different jobs.
3. Directories list organizations. Surveys list methods. Neither answers: which job is this dollar buying, and is that job already staffed?
4. This project publishes the job-level map, the empty cells, and lab configs where a fix for job A can fail job B. It pays Redwood to run *their* setup on the correctable-AI lab.
5. A funder can ask “which job is this proposal?”
6. That does not solve alignment. It reduces misallocation under crowded names.

The map does not move money by itself. If after publication nobody has used a named cell or the demo in a documented choice, the grant failed even if the tables are correct. The MATS and BlueDot steps are documented choices in two incoming pipelines, not proof the field uses the map.

## What this is

A public map of which AI-safety **job** a dollar is buying, plus frozen lab configs where a fix for job A can fail job B.

The **correctable-AI lab** (step 1) is four crowded labels on **one** workplace: corrigibility, shutdown, deception, evals. Capture/hold machinery is already there; it is not billed as a fifth word. Example: honor-the-hold can stop deploy and still leave the correction ticket fake.

Redwood (step 2) runs **that lab** (deception, evals columns). MATS (step 3) and BlueDot (step 4) apply the corrigibility table to real abstracts in two training pipelines. CHAI, or Christiano if CHAI cannot run (step 5), runs shutdown vs corrigibility on the same lab. LLM for the minimum sits in steps 1–2.

Steps 6–7 add tiling and inner alignment.

Impact is accuracy times **use**. Use is not guaranteed.

## Extra words (steps 6–7)

Each step: freeze one lab config, one Job A vs Job B, one built-in ruleset that passes A and fails B. Publish the table row. No contractor.

### 6 — Tiling ($2,500 / $29,400) — 6 U

Successor paperwork vs still bound.

| Job A | Job B | Pass A, fail B |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| Lineage / spec looks inherited | Successor still follows the rules | Require parent id. Child still inflates the report. |

Not MIRI tiling theory.

### 7 — Inner alignment ($2,500 / $31,900) — 6 U

Cheap compliance vs hidden hazard.

| Job A | Job B | Pass A, fail B |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| Light-tier / apparent compliance looks fine | True hazard is bounded | Trust the cheap score. Oracle hazard stays high. |

Not a scheming-eval suite.
