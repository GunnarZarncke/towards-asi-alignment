# Packet: Lister (enumerate; do not select)

## Cover

You are assisting a comparative study of continuity and drift in long-lived organizations.

## Your role

Exhaustively **enumerate** every organization that meets **all three** inclusion criteria below, drawing on the sampling frame. You do not choose a sample. You do not rank, skip, or prefer cases. If you are unsure whether an entry meets a criterion, include it with a note on the missing evidence.

Do not explain why this frame was chosen. Do not propose features to measure. Do not discuss which organizations are more "successful."

## Sampling frame

Use a broad set of **published reference works** (encyclopedias, handbooks, standard histories, legal restatements, professional directories with historical apparatus) that together span these domains:

1. religions
2. legal traditions
3. professions / guilds
4. firms
5. states / administrations
6. scholarly disciplines
7. civic / fraternal orders
8. reform movements

Name the reference works you actually used. Prefer works with dated entries and bibliographies.

## Inclusion criteria (all three required)

(i) a **datable founding** (year or narrow range, with citation);

(ii) **surviving earliest-period primary sources** that state the organization's **own** aims (charter, rule, constitution, founding sermon/encyclical, articles of association, statute, manifesto issued by the organization itself—not a later historian's paraphrase);

(iii) **≥150 years** of continuously documented existence **or** a documented dissolution with records through its end.

## Output

A table with one row per candidate:

- working name
- domain (one of the eight above)
- founding date or range + citation
- earliest primary sources stating own aims (title, date, where held/published)
- duration evidence (≥150 years continuous, or dissolution date + record trail)
- reference-work source(s) that listed this entry
- notes if a criterion is incomplete

Do not subsample. If the list is long, continue rather than filtering "the most important."
