## Source ontology

Andrew Critch names a **conflationary alliance**: a social coalition whose size is a function of how many distinct valued referents are packed into one word. The alliance grows *because of* the confusion, not despite it; persistent alliances resist deconflation (by design or by selection on memes that glue larger groups). The case is “consciousness”: informal interviews found many different introspected processes, used as whatever internal experience the speaker values intrinsically, so “consciousness is valuable” is widely agreeable while the referent is not. Other candidate terms flagged (not argued): fairness, justice, alignment, safety. The replacement move is to drop the alliance-word and talk about the intended split (here: intrinsically valued cognition vs formidable intelligence).

- **Overlap:** ch40 is the surface-vs-structure detector: a system keeps moral/alignment language while control geometry moves. ch11 is the evaluator-side analogue: “capability” looks obvious but each score imports a frame. TSA’s six claims already *do* a deconflation of “alignment.”
- **Gap:** Those objects are agent-side or measurement-side. Critch’s unit is a *human coalition* held together by productive underdetermination: alliance size = f(conflated valued concepts), with selection for resistance to disambiguation. TSA can say “the word stayed, the referent moved” (laundering) or “the test imported an ontology” (ch11); it cannot, without stretching, say “this term is load-bearing *because* it glues people who value different things.” Field coalitions around “alignment”/“safety” are the live case Critch flags.

## One-line finding

TSA already splits surface words from referents in systems and tests; it still lacks Critch’s primitive that some terms persist because they glue coalitions of people who do not share a referent.
