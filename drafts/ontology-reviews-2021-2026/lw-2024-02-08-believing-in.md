## Source ontology

Anna Salamon splits ordinary “belief” into two compatible, lawlike types. A *prediction* is a bet on an outcome the belief does not affect. A *believing-in* is a kickstarter: a public, often identity-bearing commitment of resources to a project (ethic, bakery, village, paradigm, “I believe in you”) on the premise that investing as if that project were real will yield good returns—and thereby partly bringing the represented reality into existence. Strength of investment, not probability, is the natural scalar; one may believe-in a modal three-hour letter while predicting it will take ten. The generating-algorithm test separates this from deception: a believing-in should still coordinate if others could see how it was produced; a lie should not.

Collapsing believing-in into laundering or into prediction erases the object.

## Applicability to TSA

- **Score (0–5):** 3
- **Why:** The split does not compete with boundary, bundle, CCI, successor, or basin, so it should not change a TSA cut. It is still a reverse-gap: TSA already uses “commitment” and “self-fulfilling” in ways that would absorb-and-erase the axis. Salamon’s explicit claim that LW/EA are too harsh on believing-in and too lax on deception maps onto TSA’s goal-laundering detectors without licensing the positive type. Score 2 would underweight that stickiness; score 4 would overclaim architectural impact.
- **Ontology-stickiness risk:** High. Pre-2024 LW training treats belief as prediction; CFAR-era confusion is the post’s origin story. LLM-drafted TSA is likely to rename believing-in as Predict-O-Matic agency, as deception, or as semantic-commitment transport, and miss “positive-sum public investment that is neither a probability nor a lie.”
- **Recommended action:** add-reverse-gap

## One-line finding

TSA can talk about world-affecting forecasts and about fake semantic commitments; it still lacks Salamon’s type for honest believing-in as a kickstarter distinct from both prediction and deception.
