# New Ontologies, 2021–2026

A consolidated list of ontology-forming concepts from LessWrong and from mathematics, neuroscience, cognition, physics, foundations of biology, psychology, and engineering. Here, “new ontology” is used broadly: a concept qualifies when it introduces or materially stabilizes a new set of primitives, entities, levels, relations, causal units, or state variables—rather than merely naming a phenomenon inside an unchanged conceptual scheme.

## Mathematics, neuroscience, cognition, physics, biology, psychology, and engineering

Status labels:

- **A** — substantially new ontology in the 2021–2026 window.
- **B** — older ingredients, but a major recent recarving or operationalization.
- **C** — explicitly ontological but scientifically contested.

| Field | Date | Ontology | What becomes a thing? | Status |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 2023 | Light condensed sets → analytic rings → analytic stacks | Analytic geometry is rebuilt so classical complex manifolds, rigid/adic spaces, and related structures inhabit a common categorical universe of analytic objects rather than separate foundational frameworks. | A/B |
| Neuroscience | 2021 | Neural population / representational geometry | The basic explanatory object shifts from neuron *i*'s tuning curve to the geometry or manifold traced by an entire population state, with distances, dimensions, topology and separability carrying computational meaning. | B→A-ish |
| Neuroscience | 2022 | Representational drift | A neural representation ceases to be a stable code stored in particular neurons and becomes a temporally evolving relation between population activity, computation and environment, potentially stable at one level while drifting at another. | A/B |
| Neuroscience | 2023 | Hierarchical transcriptomic brain-cell taxonomy | “Cell type” becomes an explicitly nested, high-dimensional molecular/spatial object—classes, subclasses, supertypes and clusters—rather than primarily a morphology/location/name category. | B |
| Cognition | 2022 | Cognitive maps as latent-state relational structures | A cognitive map is generalized from an internal spatial map to a latent-state structure encoding states, relations, transitions, abstractions and generalization. | B, explicitly ontological |
| Physics | 2021–2022 | Non-invertible / categorical symmetry | A symmetry no longer has to be a reversible element of a group: topological defects/operators and their fusion rules become symmetry generators, naturally forming fusion or higher categories. | A |
| Physics | 2022 | Altermagnetism | The old ferro/antiferromagnet dichotomy gains a third symmetry-distinct magnetic phase, combining zero net magnetization with spin-split, time-reversal-breaking electronic structure. | A |
| Physics / mathematics | 2023 | Symmetry TFT (SymTFT) | Instead of describing a theory's symmetries as a bag of transformations, the entire symmetry/anomaly structure becomes an associated (d+1)-dimensional topological field theory. | A/B |
| Foundations of biology | 2021 | Kinematic self-reproduction / reconfigurable organisms | Reproduction is separated from organismal growth: a multicellular entity can reproduce by moving external cells into self-like configurations, making reproduction a dynamical organization pattern rather than necessarily a developmental lineage mechanism. | A |
| Biology / cognition | 2022 | TAME: multiscale Selves and persuadability | “Agent” and “self” become scale-dependent empirical objects ranging from cells to tissues to organisms; morphogenesis becomes problem-solving in anatomical space and agency is graded by what intervention vocabulary successfully controls a system. | A |
| Biology / engineering | 2023 | Organoid intelligence | A brain organoid is recast from a tissue model into a possible trainable biological computational agent, with inputs, outputs, learning, memory and closed-loop interfaces. | A, partly programmatic |
| Biology / engineering | 2023 | Anthrobots / biobots | Ordinary genetically unmodified adult human cells can constitute a novel self-assembled motile living entity with its own morphology and behaviour, breaking the identification organism ≈ naturally evolved body plan. | A |
| Foundations of biology / physics | 2023 | Assembly theory: objects as histories | An object is defined not merely by instantaneous material configuration but by its space of possible construction histories, assembly index and copy number; history becomes intrinsic to the physical description. | C |
| Psychology | 2023 | Machine psychology | The class of “experimental subject” expands from humans and animals to artificial models whose latent cognitive organization is inferred through behavioural experiments, rather than treated only as engineered software. | A |
| Psychology / behavioural science | 2022–2023 | Behaviour Change Intervention Ontology (BCIO/BCTO) | A behavioural intervention is formally decomposed into target behaviour, content/technique, source, mode, schedule, style, context, mechanisms, engagement, fidelity and outcomes, with machine-readable relations rather than prose labels. | A |
| Psychology / psychotherapy | 2023 | Idiographic process networks / process-based therapy | The primary clinical object shifts from membership in a latent disease category toward a time-varying within-person network of biopsychosocial processes and causal relations. | B |
| Engineering / AI | 2021 | Foundation model | The primary engineered AI object changes from a task-specific trained model to a general computational substrate trained broadly once and adapted into many downstream systems. | A |
| Engineering / science | 2023 | Self-driving laboratory | “The experiment” stops being an action selected externally by a scientist; the entire ML–robotics–instrumentation loop becomes an epistemic agent choosing experiments conditional on previous outcomes. | A/B |
| Engineering / robotics | 2022 | Embodied/material intelligence | Computation and control are redistributed from controller software into body morphology, material mechanics and environment interaction: the material itself becomes part of the controller. | B |

## Recurrent ontology-generation operations

Across LessWrong and the scientific examples, several recurring operations appear:

1. **Promote a latent regularity to an object.** Examples: social dark matter, natural latents, neural manifolds, altermagnets.
2. **Change the unit of analysis.** Examples: simulator vs simulacrum, cyborg, hierarchical agent, self-driving laboratory, multiscale Selves.
3. **Split a previously atomic concept.** Examples: belief vs believing-in, reward vs learned target, multiple flavors of misalignment.
4. **Replace an intrinsic property with a relational property.** Examples: safety as technology × social environment; representation as geometry relative to other population states.
5. **Add history to state.** Examples: teleosemantic reference and assembly theory.
6. **Generalize the algebra of permitted objects.** Examples: group symmetries → categorical/non-invertible symmetries; conventional analytic spaces → analytic stacks.
7. **Remove a formerly constitutive boundary.** Examples: intelligence without an organism, computation partly in material morphology, reproduction without ordinary organismal growth.

A recurring higher-order question is therefore:

> **Which coarse-graining deserves to be treated as an entity?**

This is especially prominent in AI, neuroscience, cognition, biology, and complex systems, where the dominant research problem is often not the discovery of a new microscopic constituent but the discovery of the right stable causal object at an intermediate or emergent level.
