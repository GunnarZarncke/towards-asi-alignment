## Source ontology

Logan Strohl's *naturalism* (not philosophical anti-supernaturalism) reifies the investigator's familiar habits of thought and perception as objects that can enter awareness, rather than remaining the transparent medium through which investigation happens. Concepts are treated as watches: they give a clear, overconfident answer ("what time it almost is") and hide that the overlay is not the territory. The sequence-sentence is "Knowing the territory takes patient and direct observation." The intended replacement is standing *on* built-up structures (categories, projections, preconceptions) with a frame-breaking practice of looking at those structures and at relatively unfiltered observation.

## TSA coverage

- **Status:** partial
- **Closest TSA terms/chapters:** ontology trap (ch07); task ontology (ch11); grounding (Claim 3, MB9); hostile test (ch44); passive observation vs perturbation (ch39)
- **Overlap:** TSA's ontology trap is the system/evaluator analogue: treating pre-existing labels as discovered boundaries (ch07). Task ontology (ch11) is the measurement-relative version—capability tests import the evaluator's conceptual scheme. Grounding and the hostile test operationalize "map ≠ territory" as transport, bearer commutation, and "if the original problem can be restated through the proposed response, it was renamed not solved."
- **Gap:** TSA does not have a first-class object for the *investigator's* cognitive habits as investigable structure. Ontology trap, task ontology, and ch39 all target the system (or the test of the system), not the researcher's phenomenology. Absorbing naturalism into ontology trap would collapse that investigator/system split. ch39's "passive observation is not enough" is a different cut: against watching strategic AIs without perturbation, not against using concepts as a hypnotic medium.

## Applicability to TSA

- **Score (0–5):** 2
- **Why:** Useful as preparadigmatic research hygiene—especially for AI-drafted TSA prose that stands on trained frames—but it does not introduce a load-bearing alignment state variable, unit, or cut. Strohl explicitly does not argue importance for alignment in this orientation post (a later review notes an intuition about alignment research, undeveloped here). TSA already has operational replacements on the system side (boundary discovery from observed variables, perturbation, hostile restatement). Adding a naturalism chapter would not change those cuts.
- **Ontology-stickiness risk:** High on the name, moderate on the primitive. Training data overwhelmingly maps "naturalism" to anti-supernaturalism (the post's comments document this confusion). Map-vs-territory is already in the prior, so an LLM can report TSA "already has this" via ontology trap / grounding and miss the investigator-side practice. TSA includes the renamed system-side version and does not see the phenomenological method as an alignment object—which is mostly correct, but leaves a gap in how TSA *gets written*.
- **Recommended action:** cite-in-crosswalk

## One-line finding

Naturalism's investigator-side frame-object is adjacent to TSA's ontology trap and task ontology but is a research method TSA does not operationalize; ch39's anti-passive-observation stance is a different object, not a rival.
