## Source ontology
Wentworth’s primitive is the **mental tracker**: a continuously updated estimate of some not-directly-observed aspect of the current task (machine internals, interlocutor affect, Fermi runtime, prototypical example, opponent private information, reader confusion, constraint tautness, types/units, crowd engagement, or the physical process that produced a claim). It recasts the bulk of tacit expertise as *maintaining those estimates in parallel with the visible task*, rather than possessing propositions or procedures. The pedagogical move is to ask “what are you tracking in your head?”; the skill is illegible mainly because it is not looked for, not because it is inaccessible.

## TSA coverage
- **Status:** partial
- **Closest TSA terms/chapters:** task ontology / ontology trap (ch11); grounding and pointers (values as functions of world-model latents, ch04 / MB9); conductive artifacts and field illegibility (ch37–ch38); ELK/natural latents as latent-readout cousins (App B, field glossary).
- **Overlap:** Several of the listed trackers *are* TSA objects under other names: constraint tautness and “which channels are controllable” sit next to ch11’s task-agnostic capability; tracking how a claim is entangled with reality is grounding; reader/operator state is adjacent to CCI and conductivity. Ch38 already treats tacit/illegible knowledge as something that must be turned into operational, role-specific artifacts or the field becomes unchallengeable.
- **Gap:** TSA has the *contents* of many trackers (boundary, bundle, grounding, CCI) but not the **tracker as a unit of skill**. Ch11 asks what ontology an *evaluator* imports; it does not ask what extra latent state an expert (or a system) maintains while acting. Ch38 operationalizes after the fact; it does not extract trackers. There is no TSA object for “expertise = which estimates stay live,” nor for teaching by eliciting them.

## Applicability to TSA
- **Score (0–5):** 2
- **Why:** Useful as a skill-ontology for preparadigmatic work: much alignment competence may be tracking live correction, ontology drift, and selection pressure rather than reciting the six claims. It also names a failure mode TSA already worries about in capability measurement—fluent output of procedures without the relevant latent estimates. It does not force a new cut; the tracked objects are already named. A methodology footnote (ask what researchers and systems are tracking) is enough; a seventh claim is not.
- **Ontology-stickiness risk:** Pre-2022 training data treats expertise as stored knowledge or procedures; this post is Best of LW 2022, so later models may have the phrase without using it as a primitive. LLM-drafted TSA is likely to pad chapters with propositions while omitting *which estimates must stay live* during boundary discovery, hostile tests, or correction audits. TSA already includes, under other names, several tracker *contents*; it excludes the skill-unit.
- **Recommended action:** cite-in-crosswalk

## One-line finding
Mental trackers recast tacit expertise as live latent-state estimates; TSA already names many of the things worth tracking, but not tracking-as-skill, so cite rather than absorb or rival.
