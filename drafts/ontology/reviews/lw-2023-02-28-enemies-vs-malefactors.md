## Source ontology

Duncan Sabien (with credit to Aella and Habryka) splits two actor types. An **enemy** has explicit ill intent (spy, traitor, conscious extraction). A **malefactor** is anyone whose persistent behavior produces the *effects* of malice—misery, recklessness, negligence—whether or not their private story is good-faith, distorted, or mixed. The load-bearing unit is a *pattern of harm*, not mens rea. The relation it replaces is the folk (and legal) habit of withholding community defenses until inner malice is settled. Sabien’s bid: treat malice-debates as recreation unless there is documented enemy-status; deploy defenses on maleficence.

- **Overlap:** ch02’s thesis is that the main risk is eroded correction capacity rather than overt hostile agency, and that markets, labs, and institutions can form a control loop no single actor intends. ch36 defines a parasite as *not necessarily malicious, conscious, or person-like*: a process that extracts benefit from a host correction system while weakening actual corrigibility, including local metric adaptation and compliance theater. Same cut as Sabien: do not wait on inner intent.
- **Gap:** TSA does not mint the two-way *person* vocabulary (enemy vs malefactor). That is not an expressive hole. TSA’s move is to change the unit from person to process/loop. Stretching “malefactor” onto TSA would re-personalize an object the book already depersonalizes (ch01: no single component has to be malicious).
