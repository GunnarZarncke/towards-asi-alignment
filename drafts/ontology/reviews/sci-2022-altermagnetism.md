## Source ontology

Šmejkal, Sinova, and Jungwirth replace the ferro/antiferromagnet dichotomy with a three-way classification of collinear magnets by non-relativistic spin-group symmetry: (i) ferromagnets (one spin lattice, or opposite-spin sublattices not symmetry-connected); (ii) conventional antiferromagnets (opposite-spin sublattices connected by translation or inversion; spin-degenerate bands); (iii) **altermagnets** (opposite-spin sublattices connected by crystal rotation, not by translation or inversion). The new object combines vanishing net magnetization with anisotropic, momentum-alternating spin splitting and time-reversal–breaking macroscopic responses (anomalous Hall, spin current). Net magnetization ceases to be the phase-defining observable; the connecting symmetry of the two spin sublattices is. Classic rutile “antiferromagnets” (MnF2, FeF2) were misfiled for decades because the magnetic-atom lattice was treated as the whole object, omitting non-magnetic atoms that generate the rotation-connected structure. Relativistic magnetic groups lumped distinct phases; approximate (“hidden”) spin symmetries split them.
