## What a point means

A high score means the proposal is typed enough to fail a check, loads the phenomena TSA treats as load-bearing, hands off to neighboring cruxes, can be named in a mixed room, or has paid people on *this* target. It does not mean the proposal discharges the crux.

- **Formalization.** Can you write a model of *the target itself* whose terms have types, and check whether a candidate fails it? Nearby math in the same lab does not count unless it pins the outer object.
- **Concreteness.** Does the target load value change versus corruption, plurality and legitimacy, bearers, embedding, mutable users, and ontology shift in actual systems?
- **Crux interface.** How explicitly it hands off to neighboring questions: where the optimizer is, what is being optimized, who it applies to, whether present objection still counts, whether a successor inherits it, whether deployment selects for it, whether a hidden objective can diverge, whether the certificate still means something. Each note gives an example and a chapter section per contact. Interface is not discharge.
- **Terminology.** Can a working alignment researcher use the name without a glossary fight, and is the proposal’s own vocabulary stable? Each note lists the proposal’s terms and the more standard terms they sit next to.
- **Funding / support.** Paid people and named funders on this target or its owning live program, as checked in September 2026. Parent-org surplus does not count. Figures will have moved; re-check before citing them as current.

## How to read the table

Formalization and concreteness trade off. PSI is the only peer that is not weak on two axes at once. KANSI is a capability-shape bet sitting in an outer-target list.

- Checkable outer object: QACI, then PSI.
- Phenomena TSA thinks the target must include: CEV / CBV.
- Typed handoff into neighboring cruxes: PSI, then CEV.
- Standard name: CEV.
- Live support for the owning program: PSI, then QACI. CEV’s parent org is well funded and is not funding CEV construction.

No peer is high on all five. That is why TSA refuses to crown one and instead decomposes “pointing” into bundle geometry, bearer maps, and a correction process, and then (October 2026) refuses to treat even that decomposition as one outer-alignment bridge.
