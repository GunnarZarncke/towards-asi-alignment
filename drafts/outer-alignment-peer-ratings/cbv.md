# CBV — coherent blended volition

Goertzel and Pitt 2012, as a clarification of Goertzel’s earlier coherent aggregated volition. Human-led blend of divergent views into something participants can endorse, rather than a machine extrapolating idealized preferences alone. TSA reads CBV as CEV’s pair: closer on who owns the update, not sufficient as an endpoint.

## Formalization — 1

**Evidence.** No Lean instance. No specify/construct card. The primary text is a paper about biasing open-source AGI toward friendliness, not a model with types a candidate system can fail. TSA’s glossary calls the primary text thinner than CEV.

**Example.** “Blend, don’t average” is a meeting instruction. There is no object you can hand a checker and ask whether this week’s assembly output is the CBV or a different compact summary that happens to look harmonious.

## Concreteness — 4

**Evidence.** Ch. 4: CEV and CBV differ mainly in who owns the blending. The book’s process-centered stance is closer to CBV on ownership of the update, and does not endorse either endpoint alone. CBV is built to stop a specific misread of aggregation-as-average, and it requires the blended parties to agree that enough of their own view survived.

**Example.** A city council that must produce one zoning rule, where each faction has to be able to say “this still contains the part we could not drop,” is the procedure CBV is pointing at. That is more concrete as something humans can own than a machine-run extrapolation. It is silent on embedding, ontology shift, and successors, so it does not reach 5.

## Crux interface — 3

CBV answers who holds the pen. It does not answer what happens after the blend is written down.

- **Who may correct.** The blend is human-led, and each party has to agree that enough of their view survived. Ch. 28, “Non-Manipulation” (`sec:non-manipulation-ch28`) and “Agency Preservation” (`sec:agency-preservation-ch28`): a legitimate update does not replace the people whose update it is. Example: a model that drafts the “blend” and then tells the council the draft is what they would have reached has left CBV. Ch. 4, `sec:cev-not-enough`, puts CBV on the human-ownership side of that pair.
- **Plurality.** A blend is not one person’s extrapolation. Ch. 4, “Plural Alignments” (`sec:plural-alignments`), and Ch. 28, “Plurality Preservation” (`sec:plurality-preservation-ch28`). Example: a compact shared zoning rule that one faction cannot recognize as containing their constraint is a failed blend, not a successful average.
- **What sort of object.** Ch. 4, “The Object Sort of the Target” (`sec:object-sort-of-the-target`): a procedural constitution, not a scalar. Example: the output is a document people endorse, not a reward model.

Absent, so the score stays 3:

- **Where the optimizing system is.** Ch. 7, “The Problem of the Hidden Boundary” (`sec:hidden-boundary`). A human-led blend can still be implemented by a composite the council does not see.
- **Who it still applies to.** Ch. 18, “The Bearer Problem” (`sec:bearer-problem`). Endorsement now does not say the blend still covers the same people after a merger or an upload.
- **Successors and hidden objectives.** Ch. 27, “Correction under Successor Creation” (`sec:correction-successor-creation-ch27`); Ch. 10, “Strategic Opacity” (`sec:strategic-opacity-formal`). The blend is not a tiling story and not an inner-alignment story.
- **What gets selected.** Ch. 34, “The Selection Turn” (`sec:selection-turn-ch34`). A good blend can lose the procurement.

## Terminology — 2

The rename is the whole terminology story. Goertzel and Pitt moved from coherent aggregated volition to coherent blended volition because “aggregated” was heard as “averaged.” Almost nobody uses either short name. Own terms, then the standard term each one collides with:

| Their term | What they are pointing at | Standard term it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Coherent blended volition | A compact shared target built by human collaboration | CEV (machine-led extrapolation). Ch. 4, `sec:cev-not-enough` |
| Coherent aggregated volition (CAV) | The earlier name for the same idea | A population average, a social-welfare function, RLHF fitted to a crowd |
| Conceptual blend | Fauconnier and Turner: a new whole that keeps essential pieces of divergent inputs | Voting, bargaining, Nash product, “the median voter” |
| Endorsement | Each party agrees that enough of their view is in the blend | Consent click, thumbs-up, revealed preference |
| Human-led | People own the update | Corrigibility, shutdown, “the human is in the loop” as a product slogan. Ch. 25, “Relation to Corrigibility” (`sec:relation-corrigibility`) |
| Friendliness (the paper’s frame) | Bias open-source AGI toward a human-compatible outcome | The MIRI “Friendly AI” research program, which was the CEV lineage |

**Example.** Say “CBV” in a seminar and the next question is “do you mean CEV?” Say “CAV” and half the room hears an average. Say “blend” and a cognitive-science audience hears Fauconnier and Turner, not an alignment target. The contrast with CEV is easy once, and then the name disappears.

## Funding / support — 1

**Evidence.** No dedicated program, grant, or lab seat turned up in a September 2026 search. Goertzel’s SingularityNET / ASI Alliance and DeepFunding money is for Hyperon, MeTTa, and open-source AGI building. That is not a budget line for coherent blended volition.

**Example.** A grant titled “concept blending in MeTTa” can sit next to CBV in a keyword search and still be a creativity tool for a cognitive architecture. The score stays 1 because the money is for a different object.
