MATURITY: draft

WHY: The chapter is well organized and its central idea is clear. Alignment has to start by locating the bounded process that actually steers outcomes, and the whole deployed loop is often that process. The support on the page is mostly illustration. The coding assistant, the customer-support loop, the recommender and the bureaucracy are all told as plausible stories, with no data, incident or worked analysis behind any of them. The page does state limits: a section on when the model is the right object, a failure-modes section, and a "What Would Change This View" section. The operational machinery is deferred to other chapters. That covers the boundary, memory and control tests, the scoring that trades prediction against complexity, and the transition invariants. So the claims that carry the argument, that this is "an operational audit" and that the alignment object "best trades forecast power for parsimony," are asserted here and not shown. The superintelligence claims, that the boundary moves and that alignment is a chain of transitions preserving listed invariants, are conjecture. They rest on undefined terms such as "value-bundle geometry" and "bearer maps."

WEAKEST:
1. "The key claim is that among boundaries that are intervention-relevant for the risk in question, the alignment object is the one that best trades forecast power for parsimony." (What This Chapter Establishes). The chapter defines no score, gives no example computation, and does not say how to rank candidates. The selection criterion is only pointed at in a footnote to a later chapter, so the central claim cannot be checked here.
2. "several of the others shape the effective optimization more than the weights do." (The First Mistake). This is the empirical premise behind the whole thesis. It is supported only by a hypothetical coding-assistant story, with no measurement, case study or citation showing that the surrounding loop dominates the weights.
3. "The alignment object is therefore not a fixed object. It is a chain of admissible transitions" with the relation T(C_t) ∼ C_{t+1} (Why This Matters for Superintelligence). The time steps t to t+3 are asserted without argument. The similarity relation is defined by a list of invariants, such as "value-bundle geometry" and "bearer maps," that this chapter never defines. The claim that superintelligence moves the boundary this way is therefore unsupported and cannot be tested from the page.
