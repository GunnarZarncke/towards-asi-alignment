MATURITY: working

WHY: The chapter states its central claim, that the relevant boundary is a testable ε-hypothesis recovered from traces and interventions, and ties it to a concrete target (Eq. ε-boundary), a residual, a seven-step procedure and an explicit assumption (A-004). The support and the limits sit on the page together. It says the equation is a target and not an estimator-soundness proof. It says recovery is conditional on the candidate class, the margin and the handles. It reports negative and null results from its own testbeds: an all-singleton partition, a blind spot for write/read handoffs, and a shared-resource over-merge. It also lists "What Would Change This View" and sets out standards for what counts as evidence. Several things are still not supported. There is no demonstration on real deployment systems. Several formal objects are only gestured at: the weights, the invariant distance d_inv, the Ω penalty, and the claim that Δ_H > 0 marks a real composite. The CID non-invariance claim is asserted with a toy sketch. The worked examples are illustrative stories, not tests. The chapter's own wording marks the experimental evidence as restricted-setting and open work. Even so, the main claims are stated with their limits, so a reader can check them.

WEAKEST:
1. "Boundary discovery is therefore not a preface to alignment. It is one of the main alignment operations." (Boundary Discovery in Composite Systems) — This strong claim rests on illustrative cases and on Δ_H, an undefined, unestimated objective with unspecified 𝒫 and Ω, so the chapter never shows that composites are found in practice, only that they could exist.
2. "A good boundary is not the largest plausible system. It is the smallest system that preserves the relevant predictive and control closure for the decision at hand." (Boundary Too Wide) — This is stated as a principle, but the chapter never says how to trade it off against the advice to "err toward over-inclusion" (False Positive Agency). The λΩ penalty and the decision-relative ε are left unspecified, so the criterion cannot be applied or checked.
3. "The companion agent-discovery experiments are the technical analogue: in restricted settings, boundary recovery can succeed…" (Estimator Feasibility and Recovery) — The empirical support is mostly the author's own small testbeds, cited to the author's own work and to appendix findings. The chapter's own results include repeated nulls, false merges and unresolved calibration. So the positive recovery claim is weakly evidenced and does not carry over to the adversarial, high-dimensional systems the thesis is about.
