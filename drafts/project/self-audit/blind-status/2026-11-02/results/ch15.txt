MATURITY: draft

WHY: The chapter is well organised and unusually candid about its limits. It has a "Counterexamples and Limits" section, a "What Would Change This View" list, an explicit test for whether a signal counts as a value (the policy derivative and the do-intervention), and a fair discussion of how values differ from preferences, emotions and goals. It also has a clear statement of the conceptual claim. What is on the page, though, is a framework with a definition and illustrations, not support for the central claim. "Values are compressed control signals" is stated as the book's first positive model of human values, yet no empirical or formal argument shows that human values have this structure. The support is analogy (cockpit, pain, curiosity), a neuroanatomical mapping the text itself calls "hypothesis generators", and a toy grid-world with no result. The central quantities (the loops, the hub weights, B_k, the dimension k) are never identified or measured. The sample-complexity argument for a small k is deferred to another chapter. Several formulas are decorative or inconsistent. The author's own note in the "Higher Loops Are Acquired" section says the formation of higher loops is missing, and the chapter does not fill that gap. The practical alignment implications are asserted more strongly than anything shown.

WEAKEST:
1. "Values are compressed control signals." (boxed claim), under "The Basic Shift". The claim is asserted as a definition and then defended by analogy. The text offers no evidence that human values actually have this structure, and the definition is operational enough to be hard to falsify.

2. "This is the biological reason to expect a smaller K-dimensional value-relevant space" and "Why Value Learning Would Fail without Bottlenecks: this is one reason to expect that human values... must pass through bottlenecks", under "The Loop--Hub--Control--Value Version" and "Why Value Learning Would Fail without Bottlenecks". Both arguments go from "bandwidth is limited" or "learning would otherwise be hopeless" to "so values are low-dimensional". That reasons from what the theory needs, not from evidence. The hub-to-value mapping is admitted to be speculative ("hypothesis generators"), and the bound is deferred to another chapter.

3. "I(s_t;ε_t) must be high enough for control, but H(s_t) ≪ H(ε_t)", under "Why Compression Is Necessary". This is a formal-looking constraint with no threshold, no estimation method, and no link to any measured human quantity. The same applies to the stability criterion I(B_k^{(c1)};B_k^{(c2)}) > θ_stab, whose threshold is never set. These formulas give an appearance of rigour that the text does not back up. The same section also states that the compression is "a condition for coherent control" without argument.
