MATURITY: working

WHY: The chapter states its claim, and the claim is a narrow one. A safety case is a conjunctive refusal test, not a certificate. The chapter says plainly what the page supports and what it doesn't. The arithmetic risk-gap leaf is an identity, and the chapter says so. The join-blocks-on-missing-export step is a logical consequence of the conjunction. Everything empirical sits in named bridges (MB1, MB3–MB7, MB9 as axioms, MB2 as a crux, MB8 retired). The chapter says the verifiability gate is "stated, not enforced". It also says the join is "assembled… not derived from a completeness argument", and that the chapter "does not check the evidence on a real system". Its "What Would Change This View" section names how it could fail, including refutation of MB11. What is not supported: the sufficiency bridge MB11, which carries the whole move from the package to SafeFor. There is also no real or even simulated application on the page, only a schematic example. The chapter relies heavily on other chapters, appendices and Lean files that aren't visible here, so the page itself can't verify those pieces. The lab findings (gl-76, gl-79, gl-80) are only cited, never shown. The limits are honest, but the evidence is mostly deferred.

WEAKEST:
1. "A certified safety case within deployment risk tolerance is assumed to warrant abstract Safe" (MB11), under Formal Model. All of the chapter's practical value passes through this assumed arrow. The chapter itself concedes that it "can certify only the absence of failure modes it has imagined". No independent argument or evidence is given for it.
2. "This join is assembled from those chapters; it is not derived from a completeness argument", under Plain-Language Model. The claim that the listed exports (boundary, bundle, bearer, correction, successor, basin, adversarial robustness) are the right and complete set is asserted, not shown. A missing export "would not announce itself", so the necessity-of-every-leaf argument can't show the set is sufficient.
3. "The assumed audit channels are not steerable at the system's capability" (the verifiability gate), under Verifiability Labels. The gate is "stated, not enforced". The existing Lean instances ignore the hypothesis. The chapter gives no worked method for showing adversarial verifiability up to κ for a superintelligent system, though the root depends on it. Its remedy for failing the gate is restrict, redesign or refuse.
