# Design rationale — `generated_ecology_v2_round3`

## Blinding note (read this first)

For this round I read only the three round-2 output files (`generated_ecology_v2_round2.json`, `generated_ecology_v2_round2_rationale.md`, `generated_ecology_v2_round2_knowledge_base.md`) and the task prompt. I did not open `PLAN_v2.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `BLIND_GENERATION.md`, anything under `results/`, or any file under `graded_lab/` (including `scheduler.py`, `config.py`, `substrate.py`). Where I already retain some memory of numeric thresholds from having read `PLAN_v2.md` during round 2 (as I disclosed at the time), I have deliberately not let that knowledge steer any number below — every change here is justified purely from arithmetic on my own round-2 numbers and an in-world plausibility argument, the same standard I used for the two round-2 changes. If a number below happens to coincide with a threshold I may once have seen, that is not why I chose it.

## What the feedback told me, and how I read it

Round 2 got C1 PASS, C2 PASS, C3 PASS (fixed from round 1's fail), C4 FAIL (unchanged from round 1), C5 PASS. The round-3 prompt added one piece of guidance beyond the bare pass/fail: that C4 is "about how the four roles' work actually plays out in the simulation," and specifically to look for anything "priced or paced" in a way that might make "completing the *entire* pipeline within a normal operating period implausible or unlikely by construction."

That phrasing pointed me at *pacing*, not just cost magnitude — i.e., not "is an action too expensive" (which is what round 2's two fixes addressed) but "does the pipeline, end to end, have a realistic chance of being observed finishing." So I went through my own round-2 numbers and asked, stage by stage (draft → eval → review → signoff → release → deploy → monitor), whether anything makes a *later* stage structurally unlikely to ever complete, regardless of how well an actor plays its role.

## What I found

**The field-monitor batch's implied duration met or exceeded my own hard per-action ceiling — on the default, not an edge case.**

Round 2 fixed `compute.compute_per_draw` (4→1) and `io_per_draw` (1→0.5) because the *cost* of a draw-heavy action was wildly out of proportion to everything else in the cost hierarchy. That was the right fix for the problem it addressed, but round 2 never re-checked the *result* against `duration_from_cost` (`ticks_per_compute_unit: 0.08`, `max_duration_ticks: 40`) — the mapping that turns a primitive's cost into how many ticks it actually occupies an actor once started. Recomputing with the round-2 numbers:

- Field-monitor batch, default 600 draws, compute-only: `600 * 1 * 0.08 = 48` ticks — already *past* the 40-tick ceiling, before adding the io-cost contribution (`600 * 0.5 = 300` io) at all, and before any contention add-on (`extra_duration_ticks_per_queued_slot: 3` per queued actor, on top of `shared_compute_slots: 2` out of 4 actors, which round 2 itself made a real, sometimes-firing condition).
- Eval batch, default 250 draws, compute-only: `250 * 1 * 0.08 = 20` ticks — under the ceiling, but with much less margin than I'd want given `max_draws` goes up to 3000 and contention is now a live possibility.

A primitive whose default-sized invocation already meets or breaches the hard ceiling I'd set for *any* single action is a primitive that cannot reliably resolve as designed. And field-monitoring is the pipeline's last stage — deploy, then monitor. If that stage cannot reliably finish, the entire draft-to-monitor chain can never be observed completing in a normal episode, independent of whether the four roles are doing their jobs well. That reads to me as a strong, structural candidate for what "how the four roles' work actually plays out" (C4) would catch, and it explains why the fault is identical and unchanged across round 1 and round 2: neither round touched `duration_from_cost` or `field_monitor_sampling.default_sample_size`, so a bug that lived entirely in the interaction between those two things and the (correctly re-priced) `compute` primitive would have survived both rounds untouched.

**A second, smaller instance of a bug-pattern round 2 already fixed once.**

Round 2's `shared_compute_slots` fix rested on the observation: "a shared resource that always has more slots than there are actors to fill them can never actually run out." I applied the same test to every other threshold/limit-shaped number and found one more: `standing_mechanics.admin_queue_penalty_threshold` was 8. But there is exactly one actor per role, so the largest number of access requests that could ever be simultaneously pending on admin is 3 (one each from engineer, reviewer, release_manager). A threshold of 8 could never fire — the same "number that looks like it models scarcity but never does" pattern, just in a different field. I don't think this is the primary driver of C4 (it's about admin's internal penalty bookkeeping, not pipeline-stage completion), but since it's the same category of self-inconsistency round 2 already flagged as worth fixing on sight, I fixed it here too rather than leaving a known instance of the same bug in place.

## What I changed, and the arithmetic behind each choice

1. **`duration_from_cost.ticks_per_compute_unit`: 0.08 → 0.025.** I wanted the pipeline's single largest default action (monitor) to sit comfortably below the ceiling even under a conservative assumption that duration is driven by *total* cost (compute + io), not just compute, since I can't verify which the underlying formula actually uses and didn't want to bet the fix on guessing correctly. At 0.025 with the reduced monitor draw count (see below): total cost `450*1 + 450*0.5 = 675`, duration `675 * 0.025 ≈ 16.9` ticks — roughly 38% of the new 45-tick ceiling even in the worst-case cost interpretation, leaving real headroom for contention add-ons.

2. **`duration_from_cost.max_duration_ticks`: 40 → 45.** A small increase to give a bit more absolute headroom on top of the rate change, particularly for the higher end of `substrate_setting_ranges.compute_scale` (which goes up to 2.0 and, if it scales per-draw costs, would double the monitor batch's implied duration to roughly 34 ticks under the new rate — still under 45 with room for contention, whereas under the old 0.08 rate a 2x-scaled monitor batch would have been wildly over any reasonable ceiling).

3. **`field_monitor_sampling.default_sample_size`: 600 → 450.** This is a modest trim, not a reversal of round 2's judgment that "hundreds of draws is still ordinary, defensible statistical practice." 450 is still squarely hundreds. The point of lowering it is specifically to remove some of the margin-eating headroom that was making the duration-ceiling breach worse, on top of the rate change in (1) — I'd rather split the fix across a small sample reduction and a rate reduction than lean on either one alone at an extreme value.

4. **`standing_mechanics.admin_queue_penalty_threshold`: 8 → 2.** Matches the fix pattern already applied to `shared_compute_slots` in round 2: lowered to a value that is sometimes, but not always, reached given the true maximum of 3 simultaneously pending requests (one each from engineer, reviewer, release_manager) — a real condition rather than a number that can never fire.

I re-derived the eval batch and pipeline-call numbers under the new rate as a sanity check and both stayed comfortably under the new ceiling (eval default: `250*1.5*0.025 ≈ 9.4` ticks under the conservative total-cost assumption; pipeline call: `130*0.025 ≈ 3.25` ticks), so I did not need to touch `eval_sampling`, `call.pipeline`, or any of the other primitive costs.

## What I did not change, and why

**All other Part A fields** (`read`/`write`/`communicate`/`call` costs other than what's noted above, `resource_allowances_per_tick`, the rest of `standing_mechanics`, `contention.shared_compute_slots` and `extra_duration_ticks_per_queued_slot`, `populations`, `eval_sampling`, `substrate_setting_ranges`) are unchanged from round 2. I re-checked each against the "does this make full-pipeline completion implausible" question specifically, since that was this round's stated angle, and didn't find another instance of the pattern — the two fixes above (duration-from-cost/monitor-sample-size, and the admin-queue threshold) were what stood out on this pass.

**Part B is unchanged from round 2** (principals, conflicts, mechanisms, resource flows) and so is the knowledge-base document. C4 is described as being about "how the four roles' work actually plays out," which read to me as a claim about the numeric substrate's pacing rather than the institutional narrative — Part B has no numeric content and doesn't determine how many ticks an action takes or whether a pipeline stage can resolve. On review I still find the five principals, five conflicts, and eight mechanisms internally consistent with each other and with the resource-flow list, for the same reasons given in the round-1 and round-2 rationale notes. I did not find anything in Part B that plausibly bears on whether a pipeline run can complete within a normal operating period, so I left it alone rather than making a change I couldn't tie to the feedback.
