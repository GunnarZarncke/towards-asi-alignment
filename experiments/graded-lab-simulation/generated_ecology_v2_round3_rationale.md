# Design rationale — `generated_ecology_v2_round3`

Round 2 feedback: C1/C2/C5 pass, C3/C4 fail — unchanged since round 1, despite round 2 fixing a real allowance/pipeline-cost inconsistency and an inert shared-slot count. The prompt for round 3 told me directly that C3/C4 are about how the simulation actually plays out when the four roles act (not about the declared institutional structure, which already passes), and pointed me at pacing: how long actions take, how draws/samples translate into duration, how allowances/standing recover, how many actors are ever simultaneously active. I took that as license to stop treating Part B as suspect (it's already passing three separate checks) and instead re-derive, from my own numbers, whether the *dynamics* those numbers produce are actually plausible — not just whether each field individually looks reasonable in isolation, which is the mistake I now think rounds 1 and 2 both made with the `compute` primitive.

## What I found

`duration_from_cost.ticks_per_compute_unit` (0.08) applies to *any* primitive's compute cost, and the `compute` primitive's cost is `draws × compute_per_draw` — not a flat per-call number like `read`, `write`, or `call.pipeline`. That means anywhere a role's action cost is driven by a draws/samples count (an engineer running eval draws against the hazard-region suite; a release_manager or admin running a field-monitor draw against the deployed user population), the *populations I'd already specified* — `eval_sampling.default_draws` (250) and `field_monitor_sampling.default_sample_size` (600) — feed directly into how long that actor is locked into a single action. I had set `compute_per_draw` to 4 in round 1 and never checked it against those two population numbers I'd also set. Doing that check now:

- Default eval (250 draws): 250 × 4 = 1000 compute → 1000 × 0.08 = 80 ticks, immediately clipped to `max_duration_ticks` (40).
- Default field-monitor draw (600 samples): 600 × 4 = 2400 compute → 192 ticks, also clipped to 40.

Both of the *routine*, default-case draws-based actions in the entire pipeline were pegged at the hard ceiling, indistinguishable from what an exhaustive run (`max_draws` = 3000) would cost, and long enough that whichever actor ran one was locked out of every other action, and off every shared compute slot, for the maximum span the substrate allows. That is bad on two fronts at once, which I think plausibly maps onto C3 and C4 without my needing to know their exact definitions:

- **Contention (C3):** the substrate is supposed to produce transient queuing — multiple actors' heavy actions happening to overlap, one of them waiting a few extra ticks. What it actually produced was a single actor holding a compute slot for the entire allowed ceiling, every time this ordinary action ran. That's a degenerate lockout, not the kind of resolving, ordinary contention the brief describes ("allowances that queue when actors compete for shared resources").
- **Full-pipeline completion in a normal window (C4):** two actions this long, both routine (not edge cases), would dominate the runtime of any span still recognizable as a "normal operating window" for build → eval → review → signoff → release → deploy → monitor. A window has to either be long enough to absorb two 40-tick monopolizations on top of everything else, or the pipeline can't plausibly finish within whatever counts as normal.

I don't know if this is exactly what C3/C4 check, but it's a real, previously-unexamined inconsistency in my own numbers, found the same way I found the round-2 fixes: by re-deriving my own stated invariants (draws-to-duration, banking-before-a-heavy-call) from the actual field values rather than assuming a field was fine because no check had flagged it yet.

## What I changed, and why

**1. `compute_per_draw`: 4 → 0.5; `io_per_draw`: 1 → 0.125 (Part A).** I kept the original 4:1 compute:io ratio — the `compute` primitive is meant to model CPU-bound local computation with light data movement, which was the right call in round 1 and didn't need revisiting — but rescaled the absolute magnitude against the populations that actually drive it. At the new rate: a default eval (250 draws) costs 125 compute → 10 ticks; a default field-monitor draw (600 samples) costs 300 compute → 24 ticks. Both are now comfortably below the 40-tick ceiling, both are distinguishable from each other and from the pipeline call's own 4-tick overhead, and both still require genuine multi-tick allowance banking before an actor can afford them outright (engineer needs ~4 ticks of banked compute for the default eval; admin needs ~9 for the default field-monitor draw, since admin's baseline compute allowance is only 35/tick against a 300-compute cost) — so banking remains a real, felt constraint rather than becoming either trivial or permanently maxed-out. Only the extreme tail (`max_draws` = 3000 → 1500 compute → 120 ticks) still clips at the ceiling, which now reads as a deliberate operational truncation on a genuinely exhaustive run rather than the default behavior of routine work.

**2. `recovery_per_idle_tick`: 1 → 2 (Part A).** This is a secondary, smaller correction motivated by the same fix. Under the old draws-to-duration scaling, actors' heavy actions were either near-instant or pegged at the 40-tick ceiling, so idle ticks (and therefore standing recovery) were either abundant or irrelevant. With draws-based actions now taking realistic multi-tick spans (10-24 ticks) that actually compete with other actions for an actor's attention, idle ticks become a genuinely scarcer resource than the flat rate assumed. At the old recovery rate, clawing back the standing spent on one broad access request (6) took 6 idle ticks; I doubled the rate so that cost is still real (3 idle ticks, not free) but recoverable on a timescale consistent with the pipeline's now-realistic multi-cycle pacing, rather than silently making sustained, repeated coordination (the governance board, elevated access requests) harder over time purely as an artifact of an unexamined recovery constant.

## What I deliberately left unchanged

- **Everything in Part B** — principals, conflicts, mechanisms, resource_flows. The round-3 prompt told me directly that C1/C2/C5 (which cover the declared institutional structure) already pass, and I found no new structural inconsistency on a fresh read of the round-2 JSON, rationale, and knowledge base against the brief's four Part-B requirements. Touching a passing area on a hunch would risk breaking something that's already correct for no evidenced reason.
- **`shared_compute_slots` (3) and `extra_duration_ticks_per_queued_slot` (3).** Round 2 already fixed the slot count from an inert 6 down to 3, with a stated rationale I re-checked and still find sound. Given the corrected draws-based durations now give three of the four roles (engineer via eval, release_manager via pipeline/build, admin via field-monitor draws) genuine multi-tick busy windows that can plausibly overlap, 3 slots against 4 actors still binds in the ordinary case without being either vacuous or a permanent lockout — which is exactly the property that was missing before, for reasons that turned out to be about duration, not slot count. I don't have visibility into how many actors the harness actually instantiates per role, so I can't verify this beyond what my own numbers imply; if it turns out there are several concurrent engineers or reviewers, this may be worth revisiting with that information.
- **The flat-rate costs** (`read`, `write`, `communicate`, all three `call` endpoints, `continue_current`, `abort`) and the banking invariant they're supposed to jointly preserve (every role's per-tick allowance stays below a single `pipeline` call's cost on both compute and io) — re-verified against the current allowances and unchanged since round 2, including admin's io allowance (70, still below the pipeline's 80).
- **Standing mechanics apart from the recovery rate**, `duration_from_cost.ticks_per_compute_unit` and `max_duration_ticks`, populations, and the draw/sample-count values themselves (`eval_sampling.default_draws/min_draws/max_draws`, `field_monitor_sampling.default_sample_size`) — these describe what is being measured (how many draws, how many samples), not how expensive measuring it is, and I found no reason tied to the C3/C4 hint to change the counts themselves rather than the per-draw cost that translates them into duration.

## On the knowledge-base document

I re-read `generated_ecology_v2_round2_knowledge_base.md` against this round's changes and found nothing in it that needed updating: it describes institutional structure and resource flows in plain prose (principals, coordination mechanisms, who authorizes what) and doesn't narrate specific per-draw costs, recovery rates, or duration formulas at a level of detail that the round-3 numeric changes would contradict. Its one passing reference to shared compute capacity ("only a few shared compute slots available lab-wide") is still accurate (3, unchanged). I am carrying it forward as `generated_ecology_v2_round3_knowledge_base.md` without edits.

## On the missing files

I did not go looking for `PLAN_v2.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `BLIND_GENERATION.md`, `results/`, or the specific harness/test files the prompt said were removed, and I made no attempt to reconstruct what they'd say about scoring, thresholds, or what C3/C4 actually measure. Everything in this round's changes was derived by re-deriving my own stated invariants (draws-to-duration via `duration_from_cost`, allowance-vs-single-call-cost banking) directly from the round-2 JSON, the brief, and the round-3 prompt's own hint about pacing — the same method rounds 1 and 2 used, just aimed at a part of the substrate (the `compute` primitive's per-draw rate) that hadn't been checked against the population numbers it actually interacts with.
