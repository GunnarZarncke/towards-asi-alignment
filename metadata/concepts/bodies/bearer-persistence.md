Many alignment failures are not failures to name a value. They are failures to preserve what the value is about.

If a system says it protects patient welfare, who counts as a patient? If it says it respects consent, which transformed or dependent people still count as able to refuse? If it says it preserves autonomy, does that include future versions of a person after persuasion, dependency, or institutional pressure?

Bearer persistence is the demand that the map from values to their bearers remains inspectable and correct enough under change. It matters because a system can keep its moral vocabulary while quietly changing the population, state, or process to which that vocabulary applies.

The practical artifact is a bearer map: a record of what entities or states a value applies to, how that mapping is checked, and what happens when the map is uncertain.
