Debate (Irving–Christiano–Amodei) asks whether adversarial argument lets a weaker judge select correct answers on hard tasks. The hoped-for guarantee is conditional: debate amplifies judge correctness at the reached leaf; it does not create it.

The book relocates debate as a **local truth-selection subchannel** inside correction-channel integrity — useful when the judge's correction handles remain causally effective, not a substitute for correction contraction.

**Short answer:** Debate can make truth easier to *select* under a correct judge, but local truth selection need not preserve the judge's correction channel. Lean rederives the finite two-prover game (value tracks truth; one judge error flips the outcome) and proves the load-bearing separation (`debate_separated_from_judge_correction`). Debater compute limits, training dynamics, and MB4 bridge discharge remain outside the finite spine.
