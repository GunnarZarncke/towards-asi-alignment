\part{Agents, Boundaries, and Real Optimizers}

This part asks where the real optimizing process is. It develops a non-anthropomorphic account of agents, boundaries, growth, composition, and strategic opacity so later alignment claims can target the system that actually controls future action rather than the artifact that is easiest to name.
The gem to watch for is boundary discovery: the real optimizer may be a model, a lab, a toolchain, a market, a state, or a coalition formed through their interaction (Section~\ref{sec:boundary-finding-procedure}).
The existing-work subsumption is that incentive diagrams and agent tests become boundary-relative artifacts: their conclusions can flip when the ontology or system boundary changes (Section~\ref{sec:ontology-trap}).
