\part{Correction Channels}

\epigraph{%
Only variety can destroy variety.%
}{--- W.\ Ross Ashby, \emph{An Introduction to Cybernetics} (1956)}

This part makes correction the central causal object. It distinguishes obedience from preserved correction, defines and stress-tests correction-channel integrity, and studies how manipulation, domestication, and false consent can make a system look responsive while removing the human capacity to redirect it.
The gem to watch for is that correction is not feedback: an uncaptured reference process, live handles, vector CCI, exit, plurality, and independence matter more than a system's apparent willingness to answer (Sections~\ref{sec:why-correction-not-feedback} and~\ref{sec:correction-channel-integrity-def}).
The existing-work subsumptions are especially visible here: shutdown and interruptibility become one-bit correction projections, low-impact and quantilization separate from trajectory-level CCI, and broad corrigibility becomes a dynamical correction invariant (Sections~\ref{sec:shutdown-one-bit-correction-ch25},~\ref{sec:low-impact-not-invariant-ch27},~\ref{sec:quantilization-trajectory-risk-ch27}, and~\ref{sec:christiano-corrigibility-invariant-ch28}).
