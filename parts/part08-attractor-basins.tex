\part{Attractor Basins and Socio-Technical Selection}

This part moves from systems to the environment that selects them. It asks whether labs, markets, states, users, benchmarks, and AI coalitions reward correction-preserving systems or select those properties away, and it introduces the alignment attractor plus conductive artifacts as a way for safety-relevant work to survive institutional pressure.
The gems to watch for are the preservation conditions and correction-audit evasion: selection can reward systems that consume correction capacity, so artifacts must make condition-satisfying systems spread faster than plausible imitations (Sections~\ref{sec:minimal-model-selection-ch34} and~\ref{sec:operational-definition-ch36}).
The payoff is the conductive-artifact program: make safety artifacts hard to counterfeit, useful to decision-makers, and durable enough to change the deployment environment (Section~\ref{sec:from-basin-theory-to-conductive-artifacts-ch38}).
