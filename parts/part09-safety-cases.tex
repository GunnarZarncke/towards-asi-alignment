\part{Safety Cases, Adversaries, and Open Questions}

This part turns the framework into adversarial measurement and safety-case structure. It asks what survives strategic opacity, goal laundering, scale errors, and hostile critique, then makes the remaining open cruxes explicit before the book turns to the philosophical limit.
The gems to watch for are goal laundering and the cost of faking: the question is not whether a system can perform safety under observation, but whether deception stays cheap once probes, abstractions, and proof obligations are adversarially chosen (Sections~\ref{sec:four-layers-ch40} and~\ref{sec:cost-relation-ch43}).
The existing-work crosswalks show why local truth, local decomposition, and latent readout are not enough by themselves: debate can preserve truth while losing the judge, amplification can improve local supervision while failing correction contraction, and ELK is a readout subchannel rather than a full correction invariant (Sections~\ref{sec:debate-judge-state-control-ch29},~\ref{sec:amplification-contraction-ch41}, and~\ref{sec:cci-verifiability-example-ch43}).
