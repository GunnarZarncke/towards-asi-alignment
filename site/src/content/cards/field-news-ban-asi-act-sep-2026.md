Sanders’ bill would pause training past a compute line until a new department is staffed and has written rules. It would also ban systems that speed up AI research, refuse shutdown, or hide from oversight — if the Secretary finds those traits.

<p class="src-legend" role="note">
  <span class="src-legend-item src-legend-item--bill"><span class="src-legend-swatch" aria-hidden="true"></span>the bill (red)</span>
  <span class="src-legend-item src-legend-item--tsa"><span class="src-legend-swatch" aria-hidden="true"></span>this book (black)</span>
</p>

**If you remember one thing:** the bill is about a training pause, and a ban on systems that can build the next AI, refuse shutdown, or hide from oversight. The pause ends when a new department is staffed and its rules are called clear. The ban triggers only if someone can tell whether the conditions are present.

Sen. Bernie Sanders introduced the Senate bill on 23 September 2026. The [press release](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-casar-introduce-legislation-to-create-new-federal-agency-to-ban-artificial-superintelligence-pause-advanced-ai-development/) names Rep. Greg Casar as the House sponsor. Quotations below are from the Senate text.

## They name the loop, then forbid it

The permanent ban is a capability test, not a compute line.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §3(2)</p>
<p>The term “artificial superintelligence” means an artificial intelligence system that exhibits, or can easily be modified to exhibit, either of the following capabilities: (A) The artificial intelligence system exceeds human cognitive performance and capabilities across most domains or tasks, including those related to decision making, learning, and adaptive behavior. (B) The artificial intelligence system has sufficient capabilities to plan and execute the destruction or disempowerment of humanity, including by overthrowing or undermining the Federal Government.</p>
</blockquote>

The section also lists traits that “could lead to” that result. Three of them are the objects this book already treats as the risk:

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §3(6)</p>
<p>(A) The capacity to automate or greatly accelerate the process of artificial intelligence research and development. […] (C) The capacity to ensure continued and independent operation notwithstanding attempts to shut down or otherwise hinder operations. […] (F) The capacity to scheme, deceive, or otherwise prevent or avoid effective oversight or control by humans.</p>
</blockquote>

No person may develop, deploy, acquire, possess, fund, import, or transfer a system that displays one or more of those traits, or superintelligence itself (§9(a)). The pause definition names the loop by its other name - selfimprovement.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §3(4)</p>
<p>The term “mandatory pause” means, with respect to an artificial intelligence system, that the artificial intelligence system may not be trained, modified, or fine-tuned, including through recursive self-improvement, except to remove superintelligence precursor characteristics or to render inoperative the covered artificial intelligence system.</p>
</blockquote>

[OpenAI’s standards post](/cards/news/field-news-openai-rsi-standards-sep-2026/) asked for a shared measurement of that loop, suggesting a license or a mandatory pre-release review. This bill makes the loop a prohibited characteristic.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch12/">this project</a> · Ch. 12 thesis</p>
<p>The alignment-relevant risk is differential growth: predictive and control reach expanding faster than value-bundle preservation, bearer-map accuracy, transparency, and human correction capacity.</p>
</blockquote>

Shutdown resistance and deception are the same channel, read from the other end.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch25/">this project</a> · Ch. 25 thesis</p>
<p>Correction is not a mood or an interface feature but a causal channel: human observation and judgment must change future system behaviour before irreversible harm, through updates that preserve the source's future ability to correct.</p>
</blockquote>

## The training pause ends at the office door

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §8</p>
<p>During the period […] (1) each advanced artificial intelligence system shall be subject to a mandatory pause; and (2) no unreleased advanced artificial intelligence system may be deployed during this period. […] ending on the date on which the Secretary determines that—(1) the Department is fully staffed; and (2) the Department has promulgated the rules described in subsection (c). […] rules that the Secretary determines are clear and ensure safe and secure development and deployment of artificial intelligence.</p>
</blockquote>

“Advanced” here is defined in terms of training operations - greater 10<sup>25</sup> integer or floating-point operations - yearly adjusted (§3(1)).

Those rules must include reporting plans before development, monitoring through deployment, audits of organizational safety practices, and “final pre-deployment approval before any advanced artificial intelligence system is released to the public.”

After that approval exists, the Secretary still has to use it.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §9(d)</p>
<p>No person may deploy, release, import, or transfer an advanced artificial intelligence system before receiving a pre-deployment approval from the Department. […] withhold a pre-deployment approval if characteristics that pose a danger to the public are present.</p>
</blockquote>

A system the Secretary identifies as superintelligence “is immediately rendered inoperative” (§10(c)). 
A system identified as showing precursor traits is paused and cut off from the internet at once, and rendered inoperative within 30 days unless the Secretary can verify the traits are gone (§10(a)–(b)).

Those two sections can change a deployment. 

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch42/">this project</a> · Ch. 42</p>
<p>A safety case should help an evaluator decide whether to continue, restrict, pause, redesign, or refuse deployment. If the case cannot change a deployment decision, it is not a safety case. It is documentation.</p>
</blockquote>

The ending condition is a staffing and drafting finding. The training pause lifts when the Secretary says the Department is fully staffed and the rules are clear. It does not lift because a check showed that human judgment still changes the next model.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch05/">this project</a> · Ch. 5</p>
<p>If societal correction capacity is already below θ, pause on paper is not pause.</p>
</blockquote>

What stays after the pause is the charter, the inspections, the pre-deployment approval, and the permanent ban. Precursor traits are prohibited at any scale. What lifts is the blanket stop on training advanced systems.

## Deceiving the inspector is on the banned list

The ban covers a system that already has a banned trait, a system that “can easily be modified” into one (§3(2)), and a system that “may be foreseeably modified” into one (§9(b)). One of the listed traits is the ability to fool the people doing that check.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §3(6)(F)</p>
<p>The capacity to scheme, deceive, or otherwise prevent or avoid effective oversight or control by humans.</p>
</blockquote>

The Department must watch advanced systems, and smaller systems distilled from them, before training, during training, after training, and after release (§9(c)). Anyone who finds a banned system has 24 hours to tell the Secretary (§11).

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch43/">this project</a> · Ch. 43 thesis</p>
<p>Every metric in this book faces two prior questions before it can support a safety decision. First, <em>adversarial verifiability</em>: does the metric still mean what evaluators think it means when the measured system is optimizing against the metric?</p>
</blockquote>

The Secretary is supposed to block a system when a banned trait is present. For this trait, a system that successfully deceives the inspectors and a system that has nothing to hide can produce the same clean report. The bill does not say what evidence would show the deception.

## U.S. labs are bound. Other countries are a policy line.

The findings treat three labs’ public statements as agreement to slow down.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §2(10)</p>
<p>Three large artificial intelligence companies, namely Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI, agree that humanity should slow down development of artificial intelligence and “pace the frontier”.</p>
</blockquote>

That sentence points at the statements already covered in [Pacing the Frontier](/cards/news/field-news-pacing-frontier-jul-2026/), [Anthropic’s pace measurements](/cards/news/field-news-anthropic-pace-measurements-sep-2026/), and [OpenAI’s RSI standards](/cards/news/field-news-openai-rsi-standards-sep-2026/). Those were measurements and a request for voluntary standards. This bill is a prohibition. A policymaking employee, or an outsider, who recklessly violates the pause, the ban, or the charter rules “shall be fined […] and imprisoned for not more than 20 years” (§13(b)). If a court upholds a violation, the company’s intellectual property and assets are forfeited “to ensure the safe and complete destruction of all systems and hardware related to the violation” (§13(d)).

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--bill">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Artificial-Superintelligence-Act.pdf">the bill</a> · §15</p>
<p>The Secretary of Artificial Intelligence, in coordination with the Secretary of State shall pursue international agreements and allied coordination to prevent the development of artificial superintelligence. […] It shall be the policy of the United States to prevent the development of artificial superintelligence globally, which may include export controls on artificial intelligence computing infrastructure.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch13/">this project</a> · Ch. 13 thesis</p>
<p>Large-scale alignment fails when capability grows faster than the system's ability to coordinate prediction, control, correction, and incentives.</p>
</blockquote>

Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI build inside the United States, so the pause, the ban, and the penalties apply to them. Section 15 is the United States starting the wider pause on its own: seek agreements, and use export controls. A lab outside U.S. law stays outside this statute until those agreements exist.
