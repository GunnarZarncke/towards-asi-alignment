Nick Cammarata says neurons in large models are cleaner than the field feared. Zvi lands in the middle: real progress, not solved interp or alignment. Yudkowsky’s cut is a screwdriver on the tower — not the computer.

**If you remember one thing:** Cleaner neurons are a better microscope. They are not a mind-reader, and they do not make aligning superintelligence into ordinary science.

<figure class="book-figure book-figure--meme">
<img src="/field-news/memes/2026-10-i-can-see-control-panel.jpg" alt="Mechanistic interpretability meme: on-image labels Microscope for AI through 90% of neurons have one job." />
</figure>

Nick Cammarata, who helped name polysemanticity and superposition, now says LLMs are not as hopeless to interpret as feared.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--openai">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://already-clean.goodfire.com/">Cammarata</a> · Neural Networks Are Already Clean</p>
<p>The canonical evidence of polysemanticity and superposition that convinced much of the field, including me, that networks pack concepts arbitrarily was misread. What looked like arbitrary packing was mostly a motif I’ll call recursive generalization, which is much less concerning. […] Neural networks are already clean.</p>
</blockquote>

The 2023 automated-interpretability project he started found about 1.7% of neurons interpretable by its most generous measure. The new audit of Qwen3-32B estimates roughly 90% of neuron poles have one main job.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--openai">
<p class="src-quote-attr">Cammarata</p>
<p>Superposition is the one the field bet on and, until recently, so did I. But I now think recursive generalization was basically right. That leaves one learning principle after all. It’s still generalization, but it turns out generalization is fractal.</p>
</blockquote>

Zvi's judgment:

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--zvi">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/w2tBHwNERDoSawznx/i-can-see-everything">Zvi</a> · I Can See Everything</p>
<p>This paper is solid and meaningful contribution that is in some ways overstated. Get excited, but also there are those who need to take a chill pill. […] What it does not tell us is that interpretability will soon be solved, or is about to be made into a science, let alone that alignment will soon be handled.</p>
</blockquote>

(He is "landing somewhere in the middle." between hype and Tuesday)

## What the words meant

The LessWrong wiki still states the job as understanding inner workings, not as labeling a neuron.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--letter">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.lesswrong.com/w/interpretability-ml-and-ai">LessWrong wiki</a> · Interpretability (ML &amp; AI)</p>
<p>Interpretability is the ability for the decision processes and inner workings of AI and machine learning systems to be understood by humans or other outside observers.</p>
<p>A prominent subfield […] is mechanistic interpretability, which attempts to understand how neural networks perform the tasks they perform, for example by finding circuits in transformer models.</p>
</blockquote>

Superposition, also, is not “a neuron has two related jobs.”

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--letter">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://www.lesswrong.com/w/superposition">LessWrong wiki</a> · Superposition</p>
<p>Posts about the concept of superposition - that is, neural nets representing concepts as a superposition of many neurons.</p>
</blockquote>

Cammarata concedes:

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--openai">
<p class="src-quote-attr">Cammarata</p>
<p>We’ve made less progress on attention than on neurons, and it’s mostly outside the scope of this essay. […] A stream can hold far more than it has dimensions just by placing things in the right directions, so this alone isn’t packing.</p>
</blockquote>

## A screwdriver is not a computer

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--prior">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://x.com/allTheYud/status/2110804218005332178">Yudkowsky</a>, replying to Roon, as quoted by Zvi</p>
<p>If you think there is any possible level of interpretability that makes “align ASI” be “normal science” rather than “maaaybe possible” then you do not understand what ASI is or don’t believe in it.</p>
</blockquote>

Or, as a parable:

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--prior">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="https://x.com/allTheYud/status/2110874411859595426">Yudkowsky</a>, as quoted by Zvi</p>
<p>AI guys: We built a SCREWDRIVER! Now we are going to pry the panel off the computer tower […] Me: I think that you having built the screwdriver so you can start looking at things is not as much progress toward the end goal as you seem to believe. […] AI guys in 2026: We FIXED THE SCREWDRIVER! Panel’s coming off the computer tower! Alignment is AS GOOD AS SOLVED!</p>
</blockquote>

A screwdriver at a late hour is excellent news, but still late for solving alignment.

## Do not train on the microscope

Goodhart bites:

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--zvi">
<p class="src-quote-attr">Zvi</p>
<p>An AI produces a final output [X] via some method [M]. You can analyze [M] using technique [T], to learn what the AI is up to. You could train on that. Never do that. You train on [X]. Only [X]. Never [M], never [T]. […] If you train on [T], you are training the AI to obfuscate its thinking, and defeat [T].</p>
</blockquote>

There are enough places to hide, not just the unexplained 2–5%.

## More than the weights

But even a clean control panel is still just a panel. The process that could cause problems could result from the interactions of tools, users, incentives, even across labs.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch09/">this project</a> · Ch. 9 · The Real Agent May Be Composite</p>
<p>The effective optimizer may be a composite process spanning models, tools, users, memory, institutions, and feedback loops. Alignment must identify and govern the dynamically coherent system that actually determines future action—not the convenient artifact alone.</p>
</blockquote>

The microscope, even a good one, may be aimed at a part of the brain that doesn't intend harm.

<blockquote class="src-quote src-quote--tsa">
<p class="src-quote-attr"><a href="/cards/chapter/ch36/">this project</a> · Ch. 36</p>
<p>A model-level parasite may be detected by interpretability tools. A training-pipeline parasite may not. A lab-incentive parasite may not appear in model weights. A market-selection parasite may not appear in any single lab.</p>
</blockquote>

**Ask:** (1)  Can you simulate the neuron, or only name it? (2) If you train against the microscope, where will harm move?
