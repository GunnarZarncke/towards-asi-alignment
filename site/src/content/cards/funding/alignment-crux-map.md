## Problem

Maps of AI safety orgs tell you "who", maps of AI safety projects tell you "how". This project builds a map of the underlying problems of the AI safety field that tells you "what" problem funding and research work actually make progress on.

Corrigibility, shutdown, deception, evals, tiling, and inner alignment are all terms that name more than one underlying problem. Corrigibility can mean "humans can still correct it" or it can mean "the off switch works". Funding or working on one when you meant the other wastes resources.

AI growing in capability can go wrong in multiple different ways all of which requires a specific solution. Not only preventing gaming of the eval score, but also that the defection isn't happening outside the tested scope, that rules aren't lost with the next release, that humans can stop or correct the system, and more.

If a funder funds or a researcher works on "corrigibility" but this more often means "the off switch works" and rarely "humans can still correct it" the portfolio may look fine but important research is missed. Funding and researchers follow common names.

## Approach

This project disambiguates the terms and provides clear explanations and criteria for evaluation and a simulated AI lab where each underlying problem is inspectable.

Funders can use the material to check if a proposal is addressing their problem. Researchers can use it to check which gaps their research addresses, and people entering the AI safety field can see where their skills best fit - or develop into neglected areas.

This extends the existing AI safety field mapping into decision-making material, packaging existing simulations into illustration problems, and working with external AI safety orgs and researchers on testing and validating the material.

This project shows how to identify the underfunded and understudied problems of AI safety. It is verifying the correctness and usefulness of the materials with researchers working on the underlying problems.

Most of it is the lead's time, LLM assistants used to draft under human review, and short outside contracts. The lab runs on a laptop. Work is remote. No travel, no GPU cluster, no model training.

## Milestones

### Minimum ($16,000)

- Simulated AI lab for corrigibility, shutdown, deception, and evals, plus associated decision material
- Independent evaluation by external lab (deception and evals)

### Ideal ($50,000)

Also includes the minimum package, plus:

- **Use tests** — External research labs run the material on real abstracts or cohort use
- **Extended lab** — tiling and inner alignment disambiguations in the simulation
- **Further independent evaluation** — shutdown and corrigibility (CHAI or equivalent external group); additional table and lab checks on inner alignment, tiling, and related agendas
- **Publish and maintain** — address feedback and republish; one year of upkeep (~one day a month, incorporating new research and feedback)

## If it works

This project succeeds if funders direct money more efficiently toward researchers working on the problem where they have most impact. It fails if the material is not used, even if the disambiguation is correct.
