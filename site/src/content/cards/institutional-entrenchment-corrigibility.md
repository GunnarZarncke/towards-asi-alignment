Weimar Germany's Enabling Act is the sharpest case of a correction channel being used, with complete formal validity, to abolish itself: the Reichstag convened, a required supermajority voted, and the act was published according to law. No audit checking only procedure could have caught this, because the procedure itself was what was being weaponized.

The postwar Basic Law's response is the institutional resolution of the corrigibility paradox: a fully correctable system can be corrected into permanent non-correctability, so some coordinate must be placed outside the channel that updates everything else. Article 79(3), the Ewigkeitsklausel, makes human dignity and the democratic, federal, and rule-of-law core formally unamendable by any procedure, including the ordinary amendment process itself. Entrenchment is itself an anti-correction device — it buys survival of the protected coordinate by permanently narrowing what the ordinary channel may touch, which is a real cost. The right level for an AI analogue is the same: entrench the correction-channel integrity conditions themselves, not the current value bundle, which must remain updatable. And an eternity clause without an enforcement handle behind it is a ceremonial bundle — which raises the guardian-regress question the dual-mandate-genesis case addresses directly.
