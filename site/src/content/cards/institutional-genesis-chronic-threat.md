Coastal and low-lying Dutch communities faced flooding as a continuous condition, not an occasional event, so the correction infrastructure that managed it never had the chance to idle between disasters. Because the hazard refreshes on a timescale shorter than institutional memory can decay, the water boards did not need a scandal to remain vigilant.

Escalating AI capability may offer an analogous structure, with a directional implication: if capability grows relatively continuously, every increment is itself a small, survivable demonstration of a slightly larger causal reach — a permanent, low-grade dike inspection that keeps correction attention warm without a discrete catastrophe. This favors continuous, monitored capability growth over discontinuous jumps, independent of the direct safety case for either, because the chronic-threat genesis route is only available under the former. Dispute-resolution processes, common-law precedent, and the IETF's "rough consensus and running code" norm exhibit a related, lower-amplitude version: correction built one small, unglamorous failure at a time.
